# Korea's Future Fund Should Finance the Next Boom

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

ITIF认为，AI存储芯片景气带来的出口、利润和税收增长为韩国提供了短暂的再投资窗口，应将其转化为下一轮技术繁荣所需的共享能力和商业化通道。机构支持设立未来应对基金，但警惕基金沦为对特定企业的永久补助或常规资本开支替代。文章将政策缺口定位在私人企业、政策性银行和既有预算难以充分承担的共性基础设施，包括中试线、先进封装与测试设施、大型试验场、专业工程人才培养和早期供应商认证。作者同时主张加强普惠、稳定的研发税收抵免，使企业能够追逐政府指定战略技术之外的机会。政策成效应由研发到首次部署的时间、与全球技术差距、主要客户采用、私人投资和出口等结果衡量。核心立场是把周期性红利投入长期商业化瓶颈，而不形成不可退出的财政承诺。

### 建议

韩国应为未来应对基金设置目标清晰、公开报告的独立产业投资窗口，重点支持共享中试、封装测试、试验场、工程人才和供应商认证，并以更强的普惠研发税收抵免补充基金。

Korea's Future Fund Should Finance the Next Boom By Daniel Castro and Sejin Kim | August 3, 2026 Read the Column As Korea’s AI-memory boom lifts exports, profits, and tax revenue, the country has a rare opportunity to finance the technologies that could lead the next cycle. As Daniel Castro and Sejin Kim argue in The Korea Times , the proposed Future Response Fund should finance the shared capabilities, research infrastructure, and commercialization pathways needed to create the next boom. With the fund’s structure and priorities still unsettled, Castro and Kim urge the government to establish a separate industrial investment window with clear objectives and public reporting. It should target gaps that existing budgets, policy banks, and private firms are unlikely to address adequately. Specifically, the fund should finance shared pilot lines, advanced packaging and testing facilities, large-scale testbeds, specialized engineering programs, and early supplier qualification. The fund should avoid subsidizing routine capital spending or becoming a permanent grant program for favored companies. The authors also call for a stronger general research and development tax credit. While the fund would build shared capabilities and close commercialization gaps, a broader, more predictable credit would enable firms to pursue technical opportunities beyond government-designated strategic technologies. Both policies should be judged by results, including the time from research to first deployment, progress against global technology gaps, adoption by major customers, private investment, and exports. As Castro and Kim state, Korea’s AI-memory lead will endure only if the country keeps earning it. The Future Response Fund should use today’s temporary windfall to address enduring commercialization gaps, not create permanent spending commitments. Read the full commentary in The Korea Times .
