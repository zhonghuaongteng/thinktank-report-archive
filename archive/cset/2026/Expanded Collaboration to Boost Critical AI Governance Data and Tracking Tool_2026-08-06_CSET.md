# Expanded Collaboration to Boost Critical AI Governance Data and Tracking Tool

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

CSET宣布联合普渡大学、麻省理工学院和卡内基梅隆大学扩展AI治理与监管档案AGORA，核心判断是持续更新的公共数据基础设施已成为AI治理研究和决策的必要条件。AGORA目前收录1000余项AI相关法律、法规和标准，自2024年上线后已被美国、欧盟等地研究者、实务人员和政策制定者使用。平台提供通俗摘要、详细元数据、主题标签、筛选器及全文，试图把分散的全球治理文件转化为可检索、可比较的数据集。CSET继续承担界面和数据基础设施托管，四方团队分别参与文件发现、筛选和标注，以扩大覆盖、缩短处理时间并提升可用性。机构对合作扩容持明确支持态度，认为跨校研究团队可以维持学生和研究者社区，并推动基于AGORA的分析与工具开发。该材料属于数据平台合作公告，未提出独立的治理政策立场或政策建议。

## English Source Material

The Center for Security and Emerging Technology is partnering with top universities to expand AGORA , its living collection of 1,000+ AI-related laws, regulations, and standards. In addition to continuing the partnership with Purdue University’s Governance and Responsible AI Lab (GRAIL), the collaboration will bring in teams from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology’s AI Risk Initiative and Carnegie Mellon University’s EncyclopAIdia team . This collaboration will increase the capabilities and scope of AGORA, or the AI Governance and Regulatory Archive—a publicly-available tool and dataset from CSET’s Emerging Technology Observatory. Since its launch in 2024 , AGORA has been foundational to AI governance research and decision-making and has been leveraged by researchers, practitioners, and policymakers in the U.S., the EU, and beyond. “I’m excited these teams are coming together to ensure AGORA is a sustained and useful resource for researchers, policymakers, and journalists shaping how AI is implemented and governed,” said Catherine Aiken, director of data science and research at CSET. AGORA is an exploration and analysis tool for AI-relevant laws, regulations, standards, and other governance documents from the United States and around the world. The public dataset and tool’s interface include plain-English summaries, detailed metadata, thematic tags, filters, and full text for hundreds of AI-focused laws and policies, with new data being added continuously. CSET will continue to host the interface as well as the data infrastructure. GRAIL, MIT AI Risk Initiative, and Carnegie Mellon University will contribute to document sourcing, screening, and annotation to expand AGORA’s coverage, improve and expedite document processing, enhance usability, and publish analysis and tools using AGORA. “Each research team brings valuable expertise and perspectives. Combining our efforts will help us expand coverage, improve how quickly and efficiently we can add new documents, and make the tool an even more valuable public resource for research and decision-making,” said Aiken. “This project has generated a community of dedicated students and researchers. For AGORA to be the definitive AI policy archive, we need to keep that momentum going by partnering with the most talented and passionate teams. Growing interest from users and collaborators has empowered us to combine efforts. We’ll be able to provide stronger tools, richer insights, and better coverage of more types of documents from around the world,” said Daniel Schiff, co-director of GRAIL and assistant professor of technology policy at Purdue University.
