# 扩大联邦选择性病原体计划监管核酸的法律空间

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

报告第一卷聚焦合成遗传材料扩散带来的生物安全风险，审查美国联邦选择性病原体计划（FSAP）能否在现行法律下扩大监管对象。RAND判断，无需新增立法，FSAP的授权即可覆盖更多病毒全基因组、危险性状或毒素相关基因，以及一定长度的基因片段。作者同时反对把活体病原体的全部严格控制机械复制到核酸材料，认为可针对编码危险生物功能的材料采用较低负担的注册和监督方式。报告的证据主要是对授权法、现行法规和可监管对象边界的法律分析，本卷并未完整评估风险降低效果或对生命科学研究生态的影响。作者明确把这些收益与负担问题留给第二卷，因而本卷结论属于“具备法律空间”，并不等同于“应立即全面纳管”。其政策含义是先建立分级监管的合法性与对象清单，再依据公共卫生风险和科研摩擦决定实施强度。

## English Source Material

The increasing availability of synthetic genetic materials may pose public health risks that may warrant new approaches to ensuring that certain materials are accessible only to researchers who will use them responsibly. The authors examine whether genetic components of microorganisms or toxins that are not currently regulated by the Federal Select Agent Program could be brought under program oversight. Tarangelo , Derek Roberts , Gerald L. Epstein , James M. Anderson Research Published Aug 24, 2026 Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email More individuals than ever before have access to the reagents and techniques needed to develop lifesaving medicines, materials with new and useful properties, and innovative approaches to producing complicated chemistries. This democratization has also increased the risk of malign or irresponsible use of these technologies. In particular, the increasing availability of synthetic genetic materials may pose public health risks ranging from the synthesis of novel dangerous viruses or the enhancement of other microorganisms. This may warrant new approaches to ensuring that certain genetic materials are accessible only to researchers who will use them responsibly This is the first volume of a report in which the authors consider whether genetic components of microorganisms or toxins that are not currently regulated by the Federal Select Agent Program (FSAP) could be brought under FSAP oversight. This volume includes in-depth analyses of the statutory and regulatory landscapes in which expanded FSAP oversight of certain new genetic elements may be possible but does not include a thorough assessment of how the regulatory options affect the public health risks or the life science research ecosystem, which will be addressed in Volume 2. Key Takeaways FSAP could extend its control to cover viral genomes and certain hazardous genes and gene fragments from both select agent pathogens and toxins and those that are not currently select agents, without further legislative action. To lower the regulatory burden, FSAP could apply much less stringent registration and oversight standards to genetic materials encoding hazardous biological functions than the manner in which it currently regulates viable select agent pathogens and toxins.
