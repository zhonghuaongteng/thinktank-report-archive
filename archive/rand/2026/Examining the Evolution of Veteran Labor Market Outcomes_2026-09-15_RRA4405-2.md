# Examining the Evolution of Veteran Labor Market Outcomes

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

RAND比较退役军人与非退役人员进入民用劳动力市场八年后的就业和收入轨迹，关注军旅经历如何转化为民用人力资本。报告的主要贡献是把长期结果与教育、职业转换和劳动力市场进入阶段联系起来，而不是只比较一次性工资差异。其证据来自纵向跟踪和群体比较，能够识别差距随时间扩大或收敛的路径。作者同时承认退役军人选择、地区和职业结构差异会影响因果解释，结果不宜直接外推到所有劳动者。该主题与科技创新支撑的直接关系有限，本轮仅保留为低优先级背景材料。

Examining the Evolution of Veteran Labor Market Outcomes Lisa Abraham , Christine Mulhern , Isaac M. Opper , Prateek Puri , Elizabeth Roth , Jeffrey B. Wenger Research Published Sep 15, 2026 Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email The transition from military service to civilian employment can be difficult for veterans: They must translate military skills into civilian job terms, decide whether to pursue additional education, and present their backgrounds to employers who may not understand how military experience maps onto civilian roles. These early transition challenges may have lasting implications for veterans’ earnings trajectories. Yet despite these hurdles, relatively little research examines how veterans fare over the long term in the civilian labor force after leaving the military. In this report, the authors examine how military service connects to earnings in the civilian labor market by tracking veterans’ and non-veterans’ labor market outcomes eight years after entering the civilian workforce. The authors linked data from the Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics program—the largest longitudinal panel of U.S. worker employment—to data from the American Community Survey to follow workers’ earnings from 2005 to 2019. The resulting dataset allows the authors to account for factors that are usually unavailable in administrative data, such as characteristics of a worker’s initial occupation. The authors used these data to compare veterans’ and non-veterans’ earnings outcomes over time while accounting for observable differences. Key Takeaways In this sample, veterans’ earnings grew faster than comparable non-veterans’ over the eight years after labor market entry. Although veterans started with slightly lower earnings, their earnings grew about 10 percentage points more than non-veterans’ over that period. This earnings growth advantage was present across demographic groups, including male, female, Black, Asian, Hispanic, and White veterans. More research—especially datasets that link veterans’ military service histories to comprehensive civilian employment records, including federal employment—is needed to comprehensively understand veterans’ military-to-civilian labor market transition.
