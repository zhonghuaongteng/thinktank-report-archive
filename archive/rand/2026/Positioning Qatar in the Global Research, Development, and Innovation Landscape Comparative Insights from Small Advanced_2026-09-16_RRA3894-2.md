# 卡塔尔在全球研发与创新格局中的定位

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

RAND将卡塔尔与其他小型先进经济体比较，评估其研发投入、人才、科研机构和创新转化能力。报告认为卡塔尔已形成较强的基础设施和政策雄心，但研发经费占GDP比重、STEM人才供给、企业参与和成果转化仍存在缺口。其核心机制是资源投入若不能与长期人才管道、大学—产业合作和多元化融资结合，就难以形成可持续创新生态。作者建议围绕科研人才、应用研究、创业支持和产业协同建立连续的政策组合。比较分析也承认小型经济体的资源禀赋和治理条件不同，卡塔尔不能简单复制他国项目。

### 建议

政策含义：将人才、应用研究、创业支持和大学—产业协同作为连续组合，避免只以投入规模衡量创新能力。

## English Source Material

Qatar aims to strengthen its research, development, and innovation (RDI) ecosystem. This report benchmarks Qatar against other small advanced economies, examines international policy practices, and evaluates its national strategies. It identifies weak STEM interest, fragmented researcher support, retention challenges linked to short visas, and limited industry–academia collaboration, and sets out policy options to address these gaps. Positioning Qatar in the Global Research, Development, and Innovation Landscape Comparative Insights from Small Advanced Economies Mehdi Shiva , James Besse , Stella Harrison , Miguel Subosa , Ishita Puri , Paul Khullar , Abdellahi Hussein , Sara Zikri Research Published Sep 16, 2026 Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email Qatar has articulated a bold vision to strengthen its research, development, and innovation (RDI) ecosystem, as set out in the Third National Development Strategy and the Qatar Research Development and Innovation Council's 2030 Strategy. These strategies set ambitious targets for expanding the RDI talent pool, raising government investment in research and innovation, and improving outputs such as high-impact publications, patents and start-ups. This report assesses the current state of Qatar's RDI ecosystem by benchmarking performance indicators against other small, advanced economies, examining international policy practices, and evaluating the effects of Qatar's national strategies on research and innovation outcomes. It highlights areas of relative strength and identifies policy options to address persistent gaps. The most significant challenge identified is the relatively low level of interest and attainment in STEM disciplines among Qatari students, which weakens the domestic talent pipeline. Although national strategies recognise the importance of STEM education, a comprehensive and coordinated action plan is lacking. Support for early-career researchers is also fragmented, and retention of skilled international talent is constrained by short visa durations and limited employment flexibility. Collaboration between research institutions and the private sector remains limited, restricting the translation of knowledge into innovation. The study finds that the Qatar National Vision 2030 has positively influenced research and innovation outputs, even amid regional and global disruptions, and the more recent QRDI 2030 Strategy shows early signs of effectiveness, though it is too soon to assess its full impact. Future evaluations should incorporate broader indicators and longitudinal data. Key Takeaways Industry–academia collaboration is limited Linkages between research institutions and the private sector remain weak, constraining the translation of knowledge into innovation. While the business set-up process is relatively streamlined, set-up costs have been high relative to comparator economies, standing at 6.3% of GNI per capita in 2019, though recent developments suggest significant reductions. Against a backdrop of declining total factor productivity, revitalising the private sector through entrepreneurship and innovation is increasingly important. Existing infrastructure such as QSTP and Education City offers a foundation on which closer co-location and coordination between academia and industry can be built. Researcher development and retention need targeted attention Support for early-career researchers is fragmented and administered largely by individual universities, producing variable training quality. Recent visa reforms, including the Mustaqel visa and the abandonment of the Kafala system, have improved conditions for migrant researchers, but short visa durations and limited employment flexibility continue to push international talent away, often before researchers can secure subsequent employment. STEM interest and attainment remain weak The relatively low level of interest and attainment in STEM disciplines among Qatari students weakens the domestic talent pipeline and limits capacity to meet future workforce demands. Despite improvements in attainment over the past decade, Qatar continues to underperform in science and mathematics relative to other small, advanced economies. National strategies acknowledge STEM's importance, but no concrete, coordinated action plan is in place. Recommendations A more coordinated approach to STEM education Qatar may wish to consider a coordinated STEM action plan spanning early to adult education, with clearer roles and stronger teacher development. Finland and New Zealand's teacher-led communities of practice offer useful reference points. Supporting researcher development and retention There may be value in exploring a nationally administered fellowship scheme for early-career researchers, drawing on Singapore's approach, alongside greater visa flexibility informed by models such as Ireland's. Deepening industry–academia collaboration Building on QSTP and Education City, Qatar could consider cluster-based approaches, with QRDI well placed to help coordinate and evaluate support.
