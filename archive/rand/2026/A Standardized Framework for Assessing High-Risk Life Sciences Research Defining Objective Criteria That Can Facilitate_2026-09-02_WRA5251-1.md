# 高风险生命科学研究需要客观可扩展的审查框架

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

这份工作论文回应美国2025年行政令和2026年高风险生命科学研究政策对危险功能获得研究提出的资助禁令与审查要求。RAND将审查拆分为实验室风险与研究收益、重大负面与正面社会后果等可定义因素，并通过决策树形成较透明的适用性判断。框架旨在减少不同机构、项目和审查者使用模糊标准造成的不一致，也为在大规模科研资助组合中筛选高风险项目提供共同语言。报告进一步测试用大语言模型按相同标准预审项目，目标是提高处理量并标记需要专家讨论的案例。作者并未把AI设定为最终裁决者，而是把它放在专家人类审议之前的筛查位置。该成果已通过RAND研究质量保证，但仍标注为未经正式编辑的工作论文，因此框架的实际一致性和误判率仍需在机构实施中验证。

### 建议

报告建议OSTP和联邦生命科学资助机构采用该危险功能获得研究评估框架，形成公开、可重复的实施指引；同时用大语言模型对现有和新增项目组合进行规模化初筛，把具体风险因素与置信度提交专家复核。高风险研究的资助禁止和持续审查应建立在同一套风险—收益与社会后果标准上。

It is meant to aid in determining the applicability of dangerous gain-of-function (DGOF) research in a transparent and reproducible manner. We propose that a preliminary risk/benefit analysis, followed by an assessment of the significant societal consequences resulting from an incident, is necessary to evaluate whether proposed work qualifies as DGOF. The aims of this report are therefore two-fold: 1. To develop a granular research assessment framework that can be applied by all entities involved in proposing, funding, evaluating, and conducting life sciences research – throughout the research life cycle – to assess its risks and benefits and to harmonize determinations of DGOF research prohibitions by all parties; and 2. The overall goal is to improve the validity, reliability, and transparency of potential high-risk life sciences research reviews. Introduction On May 5, 2025, the White House issued Executive Order (EO) 14292, Improving the Safety and Security of Biological Research, which prohibited federal funding for a category of biological experimentation called dangerous gain-of-function (DGOF) research. It also instructed the federal government to “develop and implement a strategy to govern, limit, and track dangerous gain-of-function research across the United States that occurs without Federal funding and other life-science research that could cause significant societal consequences.” On July 20, 2026, the White House issued United States Government Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences Research, which together with the EO, establish funding prohibitions and research review requirements that will shape the future of life sciences research involving biological agents. 4 The policy creates a few key considerations for whether research is DGOF: 1. It must grow or manipulate a biological agent; 2. It must meet seek, achieve, or has a substantial risk of achieving one or more of the outcomes listed in the policy;5 and 3. It must have the potential to result in significant negative societal consequences (SNSC) that are not outweighed by significant positive societal consequences (SPSC)6. Further clarification is provided in the 2026 policy, compared to the EO, that SPSC is primarily embodied by the likelihood for the research to contribute to the development and approval of medical countermeasures. Different interpretations of what constitutes the potential to result in significant societal consequences (SSC) may lead to different interpretations regarding the applicability of DGOF prohibitions to specific research projects. For instance, subject-matter experts had difficulty achieving unanimity in their determinations about whether a previously published work is DGOF. 7 Additionally, after the National Institutes of Health (NIH) published two Notices asking researchers to self-identify research that met the DGOF definition,8 there were reportedly very few extramural researchers who self-identified that their work was covered by the executive order. 9 NIH assessed their portfolio and cancelled approximately 40 research grants in July of 2025, with an additional 172 reportedly under consideration for being cancelled for meeting the DGOF criteria. 10 Scientists criticized this decision, stating that cancelled projects did not pose real risks to the public, either because they were studying infectious agents that could not pose such risks (e.g., Mycobacterium tuberculosis) or were being conducted in high containment that already ensured the public’s safety. In this report, we built upon that method and devised a two-tier approach to assessing research for the application of DGOF funding prohibitions. We anticipate that the adoption of this approach by federal funding agencies would result in a research landscape that is easier for all parties to navigate, building safety and security for Americans, and facilitating the vitality of critical research intended to characterize and counter infectious diseases and toxins. The remainder of this report is organized as follows: • Chapter 2: Application of an Objective Assessment Framework to Make Determinations about DGOF Applicability – This chapter describes how the frameworks can be used to make determinations about the applicability of DGOF research prohibitions and potential DGOF oversight requirements. This chapter also describes how to use an AI-tool to enable implementation of this assessment for large volumes of research proposals and demonstrates that human and LLM-enabled scoring are complementary and coherent. • Chapter 3: Conclusions and Recommendations – This chapter provides the following recommendations for federal life sciences funding agencies. Recommendation 1: Adopt a standardized framework for DGOF reviews. Recommendation 2: Apply framework to an AI tool to facilitate DGOF reviews at scale. • Appendix A: Detailed Methodology for Assessing Laboratory Risks Versus Research Benefits (Factors and Indicators) – This appendix provides granular and objective criteria for making uniform and reproducible assessment of the scientific merit of proposed research based on the laboratory risks and research benefits anticipated from the work. This is the first step that we recommend for assessing DGOF research. • Appendix B: Detailed Methodology for Assessing Significant Societal Consequences (SSC) to Make Final DGOF Determinations (Factors and Indicators) – This appendix provides granular and objective criteria for making uniform and reproducible assessments of SSC associated with the proposed research. The assessment facilitated by this appendix is used for making determinations about whether proposed research constitutes DGOF or potential DGOF. • Appendix C: Likelihood and Confidence Score Definitions – This appendix provides a rubric describing how to make determinations about the scale of confidence and likelihood scores when applying the assessment frameworks. • Appendix D: Calculation of the Risk and Benefit Index Scores – This appendix provides a mathematic methodology for calculating risk, benefit, SNSC, and SPSC scores using the factors and indicators in the frameworks provided in Appendices A and B.
