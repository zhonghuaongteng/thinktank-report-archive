# 统筹基础设施、环境与社区约束优化数据中心选址

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

RAND把数据中心选址视为电力、土地、环境和社区承载力之间的系统优化问题。工具通过数据和可视化让地方政府及产业方比较不同地点的基础设施约束、建设收益和外部成本。报告的核心判断是，单看电价或税收优惠会低估并网、用水、交通和社区接受度对项目可行性的影响。作者建议把模型用于早期筛选、利益相关者沟通和情景比较，同时保留地方许可与环境审查。其局限在于输入数据质量和权重会改变结果，因此工具不能替代现场调查或政治决策。

### 建议

政策含义：在项目早期用多维数据比较选址，并把社区协商、环境审查和现场核验保留为必要环节。

Land Use 1 Optimizing Data Center Siting: Land Use Land availability and affordability are balanced by the connectedness of the site, or how easily power, water, and broadband connectivity can be accessed. What you need to know SIZE The land area required for a data center varies based on the type of data center. Proposed sites in Texas that include natural gas wells or nuclear power range from 1,000 to nearly 30,000 acres. • Hyperscale and AI-specialized data centers require the most space and are often integrated into expansive campuses. Smaller data centers can often be integrated into other buildings. • The area of the site also depends on the necessary infrastructure being co-located. Z ONING Local zoning ordinances will dictate restrictions and use cases for a specific parcel and will specify • which sites can be used for data center facilities • the processes to gain approval from local planning and permitting entities • whether waivers may be required to bypass zoning restrictions. ADDITIONAL SITE CONSIDERATIONS Beyond the size of the parcel and zoning requirements, there are site characteristics to consider: • Terrain and topography could affect the cost of site preparation and clearing requirements. • Retired industrial sites, abandoned mines, or retired coal-fired power plants might be suitable, and water and power infrastructure could potentially be reused. Fact Sheet Land Use 2 Legal and regulatory considerations • A recent executive order (Executive Order 14318) provides opportunities for financial support and expedited permitting and environmental reviews when federal lands, including brownfield and superfund sites, are used. • Local zoning regulations and land-use policy will affect use cases, permitting, setbacks, and other requirements for data center sites. • Existing facilities and communities surrounding a proposed data center site should be considered when evaluating local impact and public perception. Proximity to sensitive populations, such as children or those with conditions making them more susceptible to air or water pollution, could also have regulatory implications. Decisionmaker considerations Land-use considerations have significant crossover with water, power, and broadband access and with local governance and policy decisions. C OORDINATION • Coordinate with local zoning and land-use points of contact early in the planning phases. RISK ASSESSMENT • Assess risks associated with zoning and the land-use permitting exemptions or waivers that may be required. PERMITTING • Identify the major permitting requirements for the site in question. Consider the longest-lead permitting processes and evaluate ways to expedite those processes that are still responsive to community needs and interests. P ROXIMATE LAND USERS • Assess how data centers could affect surrounding landowners and other community activities and populations.
