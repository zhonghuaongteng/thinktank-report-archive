# China's Retirement Reform and the 2035 Statutory Working-Age Window: Buying Time amid Demographic Decline and AI-Driven Change

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

RAND判断，中国2025年延迟退休改革只能形成暂时的法定劳动年龄窗口，无法逆转人口老龄化，政策价值取决于新增法定劳动人口能否转化为实际就业和生产率。月度出生队列模拟显示，中央情景下2024至2035年法定劳动年龄人口可增加约550万，而旧制度下将减少约4300万；相较旧制度，2030年和2035年分别多保留约2800万和4800万人。缓冲效应在2040年代前中期达到约6600万至7900万人的峰值，此后仍会随人口结构继续衰减。女性贡献约占2035年效应的54%，其中旧制度下50岁退休的蓝领女性单独贡献约38%，说明政策成本和就业吸收压力高度集中。作者对增长效果持明显克制态度：即使假定所有保留人员都就业，改革对2035年前年度增长的提升也仅约0.3个百分点，远低于实现2035年收入目标所需约4.17%的平均增速。报告还指出，AI究竟补充还是替代这些以生产、服务和体力岗位为主的劳动者尚无定论，模型测算的是法定人口窗口，并未证明其必然形成经济能力。

### 中国/上海参考

报告把中国延迟退休的政策效应限定为“争取时间”，并明确提出后续观察变量：高龄劳动参与率、雇主需求、劳动密集型服务业吸收能力、生产率变化，以及AI对蓝领女性等主要保留群体的岗位替代或增强效应。

Using a monthly birth-cohort simulation, this report estimates the reform’s effect through 2050 and examines what would be required for the additional statutory headcount to become productive economic capacity. In the preferred scenario, the reform keeps the statutory working-age population broadly stable through the mid- 2030s and leaves about 48 million more people below statutory retirement thresholds in 2035 than under the old rules. Its direct contribution to growth is limited, however, and its broader value depends on older-worker participation, employer demand, productivity, service-sector absorption, and how artificial intelligence affects the occupations represented among the retained cohorts. • The reform creates a temporary statutory working-age window, not a solution to aging. In the preferred central scenario, it holds China’s statutory working-age population roughly flat through the mid-2030s—a rise of about 5.5 million between 2024 and 2035, against a decline of roughly 43 million under the old rules. Relative to the old rules, it keeps about 28 million additional people below the statutory retirement threshold by 2030 and about 48 million by 2035. • The period of broad stability ends after the mid-2030s; the reform effect itself remains large into the 2040s. The reform delays the decline in the working-age population but does not reverse it. The magnitude of the buffering effect peaks in the early-to-mid 2040s at roughly 66 to 79 million additional people depending on specification, and then it erodes as the statutory population resumes a sustained decline. This quantity refers to statutory headcount, not realized employment. • The cushion depends disproportionately on extending the working lives of blue-collar women. Women account for roughly 54 percent of the 2035 effect. Blue-collar women on the lower-wage “old-50” track, who receive the reform’s largest increase of five years, supply about 38 percent of the total. • The reform’s direct contribution to growth is small because labor had already stopped driving Chinese economic growth. Over 2016–2023, labor contributed essentially nothing to GDP growth while capital and productivity carried almost all of it. Even on the generous assumption that every retained person works, the reform would leave annual growth through 2035 only about 0.3 percentage points higher than it would be under the old rules. This is a minuscule number relative to the roughly 4.17 percent average annual GDP growth target that Beijing has identified as necessary to achieve its 2035 income target. • Whether the window becomes true economic capacity depends on productivity, demand, and AI’s effect on the retained workers. Meeting the 2035 target increasingly falls on productivity as capital’s contribution fades. Many women assigned to the old-50 proxy are in frontline, production, service, or manual occupation groups. Whether AI complements or substitutes for tasks prevalent in those occupations is an open empirical v question that this model does not estimate; the report quantifies the window itself, not what AI does with it. The reform is therefore a complement to, but not a substitute for, China’s productivity agenda. Main Claim: A Window, Not a Solution China’s pool of working-age people is shrinking. That decline weighs directly on economic growth, and the impact deepens over time. Beijing has been searching for policy levers that can slow the loss. China’s January 2025 retirement reform creates a temporary statutory working-age (SWA) window through 2035. It buys time, but it does not solve population aging by itself. China’s historic population decline reflects both past policies and current economic constraints. Decades of birth restrictions, including the one-child policy, reduced the size of younger cohorts and reshaped family norms; urbanization and the rising cost of raising children have further discouraged births even after birth limits were relaxed (“China’s Population Drops for Fourth Year as Fewer Babies Born,” 2026). China has one of the world’s highest childrearing costs relative to gross domestic product (GDP) per capita (“China’s Childcare Costs Among Highest in World—Think Tank,” 2024). In 2025, births fell to 7.92 million while deaths reached 11.31 million, marking China’s fourth consecutive year of population decline (“China’s Population Drops for Fourth Year as Fewer Babies Born,” 2026). These conditions have produced an increasingly top-heavy population pyramid, with smaller cohorts of future labor- market entrants and growing older age groups (Figure 1.1). Fewer young cohorts will enter the labor market, and larger older cohorts place rising pressure on pensions, health care, and elder care. 2 Figure 1.1. In response to these pressures, China adopted a retirement reform in September 2024 that took effect in January 2025, raising some of the world’s lowest statutory retirement ages. Over a nearly 15-year phase-in, men’s retirement age rises from 60 to 63, women in white-collar tracks from 55 to 58, and women in blue-collar tracks from 50 to 55 (Standing Committee of the National People’s Congress, 2024; “China Approves Plan to Raise Retirement Age from January 2025,” 2024). 1 A monthly birth-cohort simulation of the reform’s effects on the SWA population shows that these increases create a potential buffer for China’s aging population. By keeping tens of millions of additional people below the statutory retirement threshold through roughly 2035, the policy buys Beijing time (Figure 1.2). For men and women in the old 55-year track, the statutory retirement age is delayed by one month for every four months of birth cohort, capped at a 36-month increase; for women in the old 50-year track, it is delayed by one month for every two months of birth cohort, capped at a 60-month increase. Although the birth-cohort ramp reaches the full new thresholds over roughly 10–12 birth years depending on track, the affected cohorts reach retirement between 2025 and 2039, so the reform is commonly described as a roughly 15-year phase-in.
