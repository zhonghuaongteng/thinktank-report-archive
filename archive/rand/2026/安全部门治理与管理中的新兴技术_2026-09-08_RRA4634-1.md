# 安全部门治理与管理中的新兴技术

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

RAND Europe考察人工智能、生物识别、自动决策、数字身份、区块链、卫星影像、社交媒体监测、无人机等九类技术在安全部门治理与管理中的应用。报告认为，这些工具可提高效率、态势感知、透明度和可追溯性，但也会带来隐私与数据保护、算法偏差、黑箱决策、网络安全、数据主权、数字排斥和供应商依赖等跨场景风险。通过文献和访谈，作者把技术成败的关键从单个产品性能转向整个生命周期的制度安排，包括用户共同设计、情境适配、数据治理、多层人工监督、内外部问责和持续培训。报告特别反对把“人在回路”当作充分保障，指出自动化偏见、干预时间和责任归属仍须具体设计；其研究为范围性梳理，不能替代对具体系统的效果评估。

### 建议

应让技术供应商、政府与非政府安全服务主体及受影响社区共同参与，建立可跨脆弱情境使用的风险评估模型，并把良好治理要求写入采购、部署、审计、复盘和培训全过程。

## English Source Material

RAND Europe conducted a scoping study to explore the use of technologies in security sector governance and management (SSG/M) with the aim of examining opportunities and risks stemming from use cases identified through the research, as well as identifying lessons learned and good practices to foster good-governance efforts when leveraging technological solutions in the security sector. Novel technologies for security-sector governance and management Pauline Paillé , Sarah Winder , Elizabeth Moisan Research Published Sep 8, 2026 Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email RAND Europe conducted a scoping study on the implications of novel technologies for security-sector governance and management (SSG/M) supported by the Security and Stabilisation Department of the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO). The study explored how emerging solutions such as artificial intelligence (AI), biometrics, machine learning and enhanced surveillance systems are being adopted by security-sector actors and how their use affects public acceptability and consent, institutional accountability, financial sustainability and ethics. Through a targeted literature review and semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders, the study investigated how technologies can strengthen efficiency, effectiveness and transparency across SSG/M while introducing significant risks, including privacy and data-protection concerns, algorithmic bias, opaque decision making, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and challenges related to data sovereignty and reliance on private suppliers. The study identified cross-cutting lessons and good practices to guide responsible adoption and foster good governance by emphasising early engagement with end users, adaptation to local context, clear data governance, layered human oversight of automated processes, complementary internal policies and external regulation, and continuous training. Key Takeaways Exploring novel technologies and their impact on SSG/M Nine technology areas were identified as being increasingly used in SSG/M: Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality (AR/VR), Automated decision support, Biometrics, Blockchain, Digital ID platforms, Data management solutions, Satellite imagery, Social media monitoring, and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). These solutions can provide gains in efficiency, effectiveness, situational awareness, and, where designed transparently, strengthened accountability and traceability. However, there are also cross-cutting risks to consider: privacy and data-protection concerns, algorithmic bias and opaque models, cybersecurity and data-sovereignty vulnerabilities, exclusion of digitally marginalised groups, and diluted accountability when private vendors or automated systems shape decisions. Enhancing good-governance practices for the use of technology in SSG/M Six cross-cutting practices were identified to embed governance into the full technology lifecycle in the context of SSG/M: 1. Co-design with end users and communities to ensure fit-for-purpose tools and accountability 2. Justify and adapt adoption to local context, infrastructure and perceptions, anticipating misuse 3. Assess need and balance risk appetite and tolerance, including lifecycle costs, while governing data provenance, ownership and cross-border sharing 4. Move beyond human-in-the-loop to multilayered oversight that counters automation bias, allows time to intervene and clarifies responsibility 5. Pair internal policies with external regulation and oversight (transparent approvals, logging/auditing, public consultation and after-action reviews) for accountability 6. Provide continuous and dedicated training for operators and leaders to support the use of technologies in SSG/M. Recommendations There is a need to bring key stakeholders together, including private-sector companies developing technological solutions as well as state and non-state actors involved in the delivery and management of security services. Relevant actors from adjacent sectors who are also using these technologies, such as humanitarian or disaster-response bodies, could also benefit from widespread engagement. Technological solutions evolve very rapidly and often simultaneously across contexts. As a result, there are limited opportunities to learn from past or ongoing experiences in other contexts in order to replicate good practices and avoid pitfalls that may have been previously identified in different settings . SSG/M good-governance principles should be embedded when considering the use of technologies. To do so, the principles of good governance outlined above should form part of the requirements when considering technological solutions for the provision and management of security services. In addition, the development of a risk-assessment model could be used across fragile contexts to assess the suitability of these technological solutions.
