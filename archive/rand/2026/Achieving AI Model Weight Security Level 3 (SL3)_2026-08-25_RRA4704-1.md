# 实现人工智能模型权重三级安全

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

报告为前沿人工智能模型权重提出可审计的三级安全标准（SL3），目标威胁是有组织网络犯罪集团和内部人员等OC3级对手。RAND从NIST SP 800-53中组织出140项标准控制和122项补充控制，覆盖31条高可行性攻击路径，并通过威胁—控制多对多映射形成纵深防御。参与访谈的前沿实验室和政府代表把内部威胁、胁迫等人力情报风险列为首要担忧，而实施障碍主要来自资源配置、跨部门协同及安全与研发速度的权衡。报告判断现有企业安全流程足以支撑多数控制，前沿实验室可在6至12个月内分阶段落地，无需先建立全新的制度体系。SL3被定位为通向更高安全等级的基础台阶，并需随威胁与防护技术演进定期更新。报告主张当前先采取自愿标准，同时指出统一标准还能带来跨开发商、云服务商和政府伙伴的审计与协作收益。

RR-A4704-1 iii About This Report As rapidly developing artificial intelligence (AI) models begin to play a significant role in the economic and national security of the United States, a standard is needed to provide guidance in protecting core assets like research frontier AI model weights from theft by hostile actors. This document builds upon previous RAND work and proposes a standardized way of implementing Security Level 3 (SL3) as described in in Securing AI Model Weights: Preventing Theft and Misuse of Frontier AI Models (Nevo, et al., 2025) and includes a detailed list of security controls to achieve SL3. The scope of the controls covers enterprise systems that store or process AI model weights and are designed to deter malicious actors designated as Operational Capacity (OC) 3, which include organized criminal groups and insider threats as referenced in the previous RAND report above. As frontier labs and enterprises storing and processing AI model weights face advanced threats whose capabilities are beyond OC3, implementing SL3 controls is a steppingstone towards implementing and achieving higher security regimes for AI model weights. As part of this research, the authors developed an SL3 Risk Mitigation Planner, threat model, and SL3 controls. Mitigating SL3 Controls for Malicious Placement of Portable Devices ......................... 12 1 Achieving AI Model Weight Security Level 3 (SL3) Background As of 2025, frontier artificial intelligence (AI) model development at leading AI industry laboratories has cost billions of dollars. As enterprises and government organizations develop or adopt and tune models on their own sensitive data and workflows, the need to secure AI models against theft is increasing. Loss of an enterprise model’s weights may represent a loss of intellectual property and competitive advantage, for example, while failing to secure an especially capable frontier AI model’s weights might risk exposure to capabilities that in the hands of a malicious actor can lead to significant harm to critical systems and human life. The growth of the commercial cybersecurity industry in the past two decades shows that there is demand for information system risk mitigation as threats evolve. However, there is no silver bullet to address all risks. It requires continuous assessment of an organization’s assets, vulnerabilities, and the current threat landscape. Further, an enterprise security function must compete for resources with other critical enterprise functions. Nevertheless, shifting from an acknowledgement that AI models should be protected to an action-oriented and standardized posture focused on implementing and increasing AI model security is necessary now to reduce the risk of catastrophic loss in the future. A major challenge to securing AI models across industry in a common, measurable way is that despite the numerous frameworks for securing general information technology systems from loss of confidentiality, integrity, and availability, few are tailored to AI-specific threats like model extraction and there is no industry or U.S. government standard for it. Objective The work we present here builds upon RAND’s Securing AI Model Weights: Preventing Theft and Misuse of Frontier Models (Nevo, et al., 2024) and was focused on developing a set of standardized, actionable controls designed to increase an organization’s resilience and to mitigate the risk of losing AI model weight confidentiality from Operational Capacity Three (OC3) threats. 1 These threats include well-resourced threat actors and organized cybercrime gangs, insider threats, and lower capacity (OC1-OC2) threats. We focus on this group of threats in this effort because this tier represents a practical and high-impact target for intervention. OC3 actors, while capable, can be fragmented and opportunistic and well-implemented controls may meaningfully degrade their ability to succeed. Additionally, OC4 and higher threats may involve 1 Loss of model weights could also lead to loss of integrity and availability. 2 greater coordination and resources and defensive architectures that may require more than one year of implementation effort. Whereas Securing AI Model Weights presented high-level “measures” for addressing threats at each of the five defined security levels, the work here aims to extend the work by connecting measures with tangible control categories related to existing information security control frameworks. Clear, concise technical details as to how the controls can be applied to specifically protect model weights helps reduce ambiguity and enables enterprise security teams to audit and measure security postures. We also aim to frame our controls in the context of the specific threat vectors identified in the previous work. Specifically, mapping controls to threat vectors enables security teams with limited resources to prioritize threats and focus first on controls that address the biggest threats to their organizations. 2 Although government organizations and nation-states are addressed in OC4 and OC5, some criminal organizations may be more cyber-capable than many nation-states and government organizations.
