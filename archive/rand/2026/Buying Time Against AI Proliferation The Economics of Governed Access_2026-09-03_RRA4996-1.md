# AI受控访问政策可能反向加速无监管扩散

## 中文摘要与研判

### 核心观点

报告把AI扩散治理从出口管制、许可和算力控制等供给侧工具，转向用户在受控与无监管渠道之间如何选择的需求侧问题。其形式模型显示，政策改变相对价格后，采用者可能转向监管者不易观察的替代渠道，因此“提高合规成本”并不天然减少扩散。模拟中，若只提高受控前沿访问成本而没有同步压缩无监管替代渠道，约四分之一的相关情景出现了无监管扩散加剧。政策效果主要取决于用户对价差的敏感度、政府提高无监管渠道成本的能力，以及跨渠道转换成本。模型还判断，当AI能力价值高到一定程度后，成本工具将失去把用户拉回受控渠道的作用，所测试政策均无法继续压制无监管访问。报告因此主张以可观察的访问价格和采用行为检验治理效果，同时承认结论依赖模型参数与情景设定，不能直接替代现实市场测量。

### 建议

政策设计应同时测量受控与无监管访问的价格、用户替代弹性和转换成本，避免只给合规渠道加负担。监管者需要对无监管渠道施加成比例压力，并设置能力或价格变化触发的复核点；当技术价值跨过成本工具失效阈值时，应及时转向其他治理机制。

## English Source Material

Much of today’s policy debate over artificial intelligence (AI) revolves around instruments designed to change the costs of access to AI; this report describes a formal model of AI adoption and proliferation that focuses on the demand for AI capability. Buying Time Against AI Proliferation The Economics of Governed Access Tobias Sytsma Research Published Sep 3, 2026 Download PDF Share on LinkedIn Share on X Share on Facebook Email Much of today’s policy debate over artificial intelligence (AI) focuses on efforts to change the costs of access to AI—to shift relative costs such that AI adoption is steered toward channels that society can monitor and away from channels it cannot. Policy analysis has concentrated on the supply side of this problem, with such mechanisms as export restrictions, licensing requirements, and controls on computing hardware. But where AI capabilities end up depends on the choices of organizations and individuals about what to adopt and through which channels, and it is on this demand side that cost-shifting instruments operate. This demand side of the problem has received far less systematic analysis. This report addresses this gap with a framework for judging when cost-shifting steers adoption as intended, when it accomplishes little, and when it backfires and makes proliferation worse. The author details a formal model in which AI adoption is driven by relative costs across access channels, and governance instruments operate by manipulating those relative costs. The central question is: When policy changes the relative cost of AI access, how do users reallocate across governed and ungoverned channels, and under what conditions does that reallocation reduce, fail to affect, or accelerate uncontrolled proliferation of AI? Key Takeaways Compliance burdens on governed AI access channels can make uncontrolled AI proliferation worse Raising the cost of controlled frontier access without corresponding pressure on ungoverned alternatives can redirect user adoption toward ungoverned access channels. In simulations conducted by the author, this occurred in roughly one-quarter of relevant scenarios. Whether AI access governance can sufficiently delay or suppress ungoverned access growth depends on three demand-side conditions Three indicators predict whether policies can suppress ungoverned AI access: (1) how responsive adopters are to cost differences across channels, (2) how effectively policy can raise the cost of ungoverned AI access, and (3) how costly it is for users to switch between access channels. AI access governance policies are no longer able to shift users toward governed access channels once AI capabilities are sufficiently strong Once AI capabilities become sufficiently valuable to users, policies that attempt to shift the relative costs of governed vs. ungoverned AI access lose their traction. Once AI capabilities advance far enough, no policy evaluated in the modeling framework can suppress ungoverned access. The distinction between AI capability dynamics and access-cost dynamics matters for governance of the technology A framing centered on capability thresholds implies that governance should track and respond to specific technical milestones; a framing centered on price dynamics implies that governance should track and respond to the cost of access. The latter may be more observable, more amenable to policy intervention, and more predictive of who adopts what and through which channels.
