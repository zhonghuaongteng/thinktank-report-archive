PE-A3490-1 iii About This Paper In this paper, we introduce a new methodology called integrated strategic forecasting (ISF), which combines scenario planning, a cornerstone method of strategic foresight, with crowdsourced forecasting. ISF’s goal is to enhance decision advantage for policymakers at a time of significant geopolitical change by combining probabilistic estimates with the exploration of uncertain futures. We provide a rationale for and a step-by- step overview of this new joint method, and we conclude by identifying areas of future research. Our aim is to inform thinking in the community of researchers and practitioners working on forecasting, foresight, and anticipatory planning. In this time of accelerated geopolitical change, those who have early warning of future events can gain important decision advantages. This paper outlines a new approach to futures and anticipatory planning, called integrated strategic forecasting (ISF). ISF builds on two familiar methodologies used in government, academia, and the private sector by those who support policy creation and decisionmaking to imagine and plan for possible futures. The first is stra - tegic foresight, which is a structured process of developing plausible future alternatives based on analytic inputs; we focus specifically on the practice of scenario planning . The second is crowdsourced forecasting, which is the aggregation of multiple individual estimates of the probability of future trends and events and their associated rationales into a single crowd forecast. ISF harnesses the complementary features of these two methods to enhance overall analytical power in decisionmaking. Scenario planning assists with exploring uncertainty by creating alternative future narra - tives and helping policymakers rehearse different decisions and trade-offs, but it does not offer future event probabilities, thus limiting the ability of policymakers to prioritize among these alternatives. Meanwhile, crowdsourced forecasting offers aggregated probabilistic estimates of future events and can serve as an intel- ligence collection and risk assessment tool, but the probabilities provided lack a broader analytical founda - tion that would augment their informational value. ISF capitalizes on the strengths of both scenario planning and crowdsourced forecasting by combining rigorous scenario planning with probabilistic estimates about their key drivers and signals. The resulting approach can help policymakers better map and prioritize future developments, and in the process, weigh alternative strategies and policy actions. Drawing on extensive experience with both methodologies, advances in research in both foresight and forecasting, and publicly available information about past efforts to devise a joint approach, we propose ISF as a five-step approach involving the following processes: 1. is sue identification and scoping discussion 2. sc enario-based decomposition 3. fo recasting map development 4. ana lytical reintegration and tracking 5. an alytical resolution and repetition. ISF’s main intended outcome is enhancing anticipatory decisionmaking. This paper builds on earlier con- ceptualizations of combining probabilities and scenarios—discussed in Appendix D—and codifies a step- by-step process leveraging the strengths of both scenario planning and crowdsourced forecasting. We posit that ISF can improve on capabilities with three principal actions: 1. De velop more-targeted and more analytically rigorous crowdsourced forecasting questions, which have both discrete and aggregate informational value for the platforms deploying them and the policy- makers using them. 2. As sist foresight advisers and the policymakers they collaborate with to develop more-responsive sce - narios, which benefit from clear probabilities and rationales attached to drivers and signals. 3. Pr ovide a baseline for a more dynamic analytical system—one that provides not only snapshot proba- bilistic judgments about scenarios but also iterative assessments and tracking of alternative futures.
