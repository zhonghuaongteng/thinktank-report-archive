1. 연구 배경 및 현황
□ 연구 필요성 및 목적
○ 최근 과학관에 대한 투자와 이용자가 지속적으로 확대되면서, 지역 과학문화
확산의 핵심 거점으로서 과학관의 역할이 강조되고 있음
○ 그러나, 기존 연구는 과학관의 설립 전 비용편익 분석이나 투입산출 분석에 치중
되었으며, 개관 후 연구는 상관관계 중심에 머무는 경우가 많아 과학관의 실제
사회경제적 효과를 실증적으로 평가하기에는 한계가 있었음. 특히 인적자본 축적,
지역 경제 파급효과 등 외부효과가 제대로 검증되지 않아 정책적 근거 확보에
어려움이 있었음
○ 이에 본 연구는 과학관 설립과 운영이 지역사회에 미치는 사회경제적 효과를 분석
하고, 이를 토대로 향후 과학관 육성 정책 수립에 실증적 시사점을 제공하는 것을
목적으로 함
□ 과학관 운영 및 관람객 현황
○ (과학관 육성 시책) 정부는 2004년부터 5년 단위로 과학관 육성 기본계획을 수립
하며, 과학관 설립 촉진과 운영 활성화를 추진해 옴. 이러한 노력으로 과학관은
양적 성장은 달성했으나, 콘텐츠
･운영･협력 등 질적 개선은 아직 제한적임. 향후
정책방향은 수요 기반 배치, 공･사립 동반 성장, 지역 격차 완화와 상생 기반
과학문화 생태계 구축에 초점을 맞출 필요가 있음
○ (관람객 추이와 영향 요인) 과학관 운영 실태조사를 기반으로 분석한 결과, 2023년
기준 138개 과학관이 운영 중이며, 관람객 수는 2017~2019년 성장세를 보였으나

2. 과학관의 사회경제적 효과 분석
□ 분석 자료 및 분석 방법
○ (분석자료) 본 연구는 과학관 개관이 지역 사회와 경제에 미치는 영향을 분석하기
위해 다층적 자료를 구축하였으며, 이러한 분석자료의 구성은 과학관 개관이 학업
성취, 과학 참여, 지역 경제와 산업 구조에 미치는 효과를 균형 있게 분석할 수
있는 기반을 제공함
- 과학 이해도는 인지적
･정량적 능력을 측정하는 국가수준 학업성취도 수학 점수와
정의적･사회적 측면을 반영하는 ｢과학기술인식도 조사｣를 병행 활용함
- 수학 성취는 시군구-연도 수준 패널로 집계하며, 평균 점수와 성취 분포(기초학력
미달, 보통학력 이상)를 결합해 종합적 성과지수를 산출함
- 과학기술인식도 조사는 광역시도 단위로 집계하되, 시군구별 과학관 개관 비율을
처치 강도로 반영하여 행동적 참여(과학관 방문 횟수 등)를 중심으로 과학 이해도의
핵심 경로를 측정함
- 지역 경제 지표는 시군구-연도 패널로 구축하였으며, 소득 수준은 GRDP를 기본
으로, 결측을 보완하기 위해 조도(위성 불빛)와 전력판매량을 대리 지표로 활용함

1. Introduction
Investments in and increasing numbers of visitors to science museums in recent
years have highlighted the critical role of these institutions as hubs for regional
science and culture engagement. Despite this growing importance, research on
science museums has largely concentrated on cost-benefit or input-output analyses
conducted prior to their establishment. Post-opening studies have primarily focused
on correlational analyses, limiting the capacity to evaluate the socio-economic
impact of these facilities. Specifically, their effects on human capital accumulation,
regional economic spillovers, and broader societal benefits have not been
empirically validated, hampering the development of evidence-based policies.
The present study addresses this gap by analyzing the socio-economic effects
of establishing and operating science museums in local communities. It seeks to
provide policymakers with robust and empirically grounded insights to guide
future strategies for developing and managing science museums by focusing on
educational outcomes and regional economic effects.
2. Science Museum Operations and Visitor Trends
Since 2004, the government has implemented a series of five-year plans for the
promotion and management of science museums, with the dual aim of expanding
their establishment and supporting operational sustainability. These initiatives

===== PDF_PAGE 16 =====
II Estimating the Socio-Economic Impacts of Science Museum Establishment and Operation
achieved quantitative growth, resulting in a substantial increase in the number of
science museums across Korea. However, qualitative aspects, such as content
diversity, program innovation, and collaborative partnerships, remain limited.
Future policy directions need to focus on demand-driven allocation, balanced
development between public and private institutions, and the reduction of regional
disparities to foster a resilient and inclusive science culture ecosystem.
Based on surveys, this study found that 138 science museums are in operation
nationwide as of 2023. Visitor numbers increased steadily between 2017 and
2019, experienced a sharp decline during the COVID-19 pandemic, and recovered
to over 80% of pre-pandemic levels by 2023. Recovery patterns varied according
to management type and museum focus. Private institutions, while having fewer
visitors overall than national museums, demonstrated relative strength in attracting
paid visitors. Moreover, visitor recovery was particularly strong in natural history
and children-focused museums. These patterns suggest that policy interventions
should consider the operational type, target audience, and thematic focus of
science museums to optimize engagement and program effectiveness.
3. Data and Analytical Approach
A multilayered dataset was constructed to evaluate the effects of science
museums on educational outcomes, science engagement, and regional economic
development. Scientific literacy was assessed using two complementary methods.
First, national mathematics achievement scores were employed as proxies for
cognitive and quantitative capabilities, aggregated at the district-year level, and
combined with achievement distribution information to produce a comprehensive
performance index. Second, the Science and Technology Awareness Survey,
conducted at the provincial level, captured affective and social aspects including
attitudes toward science, interest levels, and participation intentions. To address
the mismatch between district- and provincial-level survey data, the proportion of
districts within a province with museums was used as a treatment intensity
measure, capturing the key pathway of behavioral engagement through visits and
participation.

===== PDF_PAGE 17 =====
Summary III
Regional economic indicators were constructed at the district-year level. The
Gross Regional Domestic Product (GRDP) was used as the primary measure of
income, supplemented with nighttime light intensity from satellite imagery and
electricity sales, to address missing data and capture economic activity more
comprehensively. Employment was measured both in total and by key sectors,
including wholesale and retail; accommodation and food services; and
professional, scientific, and technical services. This enabled the identification of
both direct and indirect economic effects of science museum operations.
Causal effects were estimated using a two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) model,
controlling for district- and year-fixed effects. This model allowed the estimation
of the average treatment effect (ATE) of museum openings while accounting for
unobserved heterogeneity. The analysis was conducted in three stages. First, the
TWFE model estimated the average effects. Second, an event-study approach
tracked the dynamic effects before and after museum openings and tested the
parallel trend assumption. Third, when pre-trends violated the parallel trends
assumption, event-time trend adjustment methods were applied to remove
potential bias, yielding more reliable estimates of the causal impacts of museum
establishment. This stepwise approach ensures robust and reliable identification of
policy effects on educational and economic outcomes.
4. Key Findings
4.1. Educational Outcomes
This study found that science museums significantly improved the quality of
mathematics achievement distribution without negatively affecting lower-performing
students. Specifically, the proportion of students in the middle- and upper-achievement
tiers increased, whereas that in the low-achievement group remained stable, indicating
an overall enhancement in learning quality. This suggests that museums can function
as effective venues to promote higher-order learning and support students with existing
competencies by acting as hubs for advanced inquiry-based education.

IV Estimating the Socio-Economic Impacts of Science Museum Establishment and Operation
4.2. Behavioral Engagement
Behavioral engagement in science activities, measured through visits to and
participation in exhibitions, festivals, and lectures, rose along with the number of
museums in a region. This finding highlights the importance of accessibility in
promoting science participation. Regions with greater museum density had higher
engagement rates, underscoring the positive role of science museums in fostering
active learning and public engagement. However, owing to the survey design
limitations, including sample size and timing, statistical significance was modest.
4.3. Regional Economic Effects
Analysis of income indicators, including GRDP, nighttime light intensity, and
electricity sales, showed general upward trends, but did not reach statistical
significance. In contrast, the employment effect was statistically significant, with
total employment increasing by approximately 3 percent following the establishment
of science museums.
5. Policy Implications
Given the observed socio-economic effects of science museums, future policies
should move beyond facility expansion and focus on strengthening pathways
through which museums generate impacts.
Educational Impact: Programs targeting middle- and high-achieving students,
including inquiry-based and advanced content, should be expanded, reflecting the
observed effectiveness in raising upper-tier performance.
Accessibility: To address regional disparities, small, modular, or mobile science
museums should be introduced in underserved areas, while large museums can be
developed into national platforms for science culture, hosting major exhibitions
and international programs.
Economic Linkages: Museums should be integrated with local commerce and
tourism strategies, including science-cultural streets, festivals, and programs that
extend visitor duration, to maximize employment and economic spillover effects.

Operational Strategy: Initial investments should focus on high-quality content,
while mid-term stability and sustainability can be ensured through partnerships
with universities, research institutes, and private enterprises to develop long-term
programs.
Upgrading the Performance Management System: S-MOPS should be introduced
to enhance managerial efficiency. In addition, performance metrics need to be
expanded beyond visitor numbers to include multidimensional indicators such as
learning outcomes, participation levels, and local employment.
6. Conclusion
This study provides empirical evidence, particularly with respect to educational
and employment outcomes, that science museums enhance regional scientific
literacy, promote active participation in science, and stimulate local employment.
By adopting a comprehensive policy approach that integrates education,
accessibility, and economic linkage, science museums can be strategically positioned
as multifunctional social infrastructure, contributing to both human capital
accumulation and the overall vibrancy of regional economies. The findings support
a shift toward evidence-based policy design, ensuring that future investments in
science museums maximize their educational, cultural, and economic impacts.

제2절 분석 방법
□ 분석 방법 개요
○ 본 연구의 목적은 과학관 개관이 지역의 소득 및 고용을 포함한 사회경제지표에
미치는 인과적 효과를 실증적으로 식별하는 것임. 그러나 정책 효과를 단순한 전후
비교로 파악할 경우, 지역 간 이질성, 시계열적 추세, 외생적 충격 등으로 인해
정책효과와 기타 요인의 영향을 구분하기 어려운 식별 문제가 발생함. 따라서 본
연구는 처리집단과 통제집단의 시계열 변화를 비교함으로써 시간불변 요인과 공통
추세를 동시에 통제할 수 있는 이중차분법(Difference-in-Differences, DID;
강창희･박상곤, 2025)을 기본 분석 틀로 채택함
○ 그러나 전통적인 DID 접근은 평행추세(parallel trends) 가정이라는 강한 전제를
기반으로 하며, 이 가정이 충족되지 않을 경우 추정치는 단순한 상관관계에 머물거나
편향될 가능성이 존재함. 특히, 정책 도입 이전에도 결과변수가 체계적으로 변화하
거나, 도입 이후 효과가 시간에 따라 달라지는 동태적 효과(dynamic treatment
effects)가 존재하는 경우, 기존 양방향 고정효과(Two-way Fixed Effect, TWFE)
모형12)만으로는 정책효과를 정확히 식별하기 어려움
○ 따라서 본 연구는 DID 모형에 의존하지 않고, 세 단계의 점진적 분석 구조를
통해 정책의 효과를 식별하고자 함
(1) 기본 TWFE 모형을 통해 평균 처리 효과(ATE)의 방향과 규모를 추정함
(2) 사건사(event-study) 분석을 통해 평행추세 가정을 사전에 검증하고, 정책효과의
시점별 동태적 패턴을 파악함
(3) 사건사 분석 과정에서 평행추세 위반이 발견될 경우, Dobkin et al.(2018)과
Freyaldenhoven et al.(2019)의 event-time 추세 조정 방법을 적용하여 사전
추세를 제거하고, 편향되지 않은 정책효과를 최종적으로 제시함
12) 통상적으로 이중차분법(DID)은 지역 및 시점에 대한 고정효과를 포함한 양방향 고정효과(TWFE) 모형을 의미한다.
따라서 본 연구에서는 별도의 언급이 없는 한 DID와 TWFE를 동일한 모형으로 간주한다.

메커니즘의 존재 가능성을 내포함

□ 연구 결과 요약
○ 본 연구는 과학관 개관 및 운영이 지역의 과학 이해도, 지역경제 소득 및 고용에

○ (과학 이해도) 과학관 개관이 수학 성취도 평균을 약간 상승시키는 경향은 있었으나
통계적으로 유의하지는 않았음. 그러나 성취 분포 구조를 살펴보면, 기초학력 미달
비율은 변하지 않고 중간층과 상위층 비율이 상승해 요약지수가 유의하게 증가하는
것으로 나타남. 이는 과학관 개관이 하위층의 악화 없이 성취 상단을 강화하는
방식으로 질적 향상을 유도했음을 보여줌
○ (과학 이해도-행동적 참여) 과학 활동 참여 분석에서는 과학관 수가 많을수록 방문,
전시･축전 참여, 강연 참여 등 행동적 참여가 전반적으로 증가함. 특히 인구 규모를
반영한 가중 추정에서 효과가 더욱 크게 나타나, 도시지역일수록 과학관 접근성이
참여 확대에 긍정적으로 작용함을 보여줌. 다만 조사 시점과 표본 규모의 한계로
통계적 유의성은 낮게 나타남
○ (지역경제 효과) 지역경제 분석에서는 GRDP, 야간조도, 전력판매량 등 소득 관련

명확하게 확인되어 총 종사자 수가 약 3% 증가했고, 상용근로자와 자영업자 모두
비슷한 비율로 증가함. 산업별로는 도매･소매업 중심의 고용 상승이 뚜렷해 과학관
