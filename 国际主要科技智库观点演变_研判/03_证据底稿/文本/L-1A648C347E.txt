 Research Ba ckground: Changes in the Science and Technology
Innovation Environment and the Need for Legislative Impact Assessment
 (Transformative nature of S&T innovation) Structural changes across society, economy,
and environment → deepening policy and legislative uncertainty
 (Technology convergence and strategic intelligence) Spread of disruptive
technologies(AI, Quantum computing, Synthetic biology) and technological
convergence, Limitations of prediction-centered, static policy approaches, Need for
agile, experimental policy making and strategic intelligence
 (Colligridge’s dilemma) (a) Early stage: Easy to control, but effect unknown, (b) Later
stage: Effects clear, but technology already entrenched and uncontrollable, (c) Need for
proactive discussion and phased institutional design to manage uncertainty
 (Structural conflict) “Definitive norms” pursuing legal stability vs. “fluidity of
innovation” → obstruction of innovation or regulatory gaps

 Research Objectives
 Explore the necessity of legislative i mpact assessment (LIA) and a framework to
address the transformative phase of S&T innovation
 Compare domestic and international LIA practice cases
 Propose Korea-specific S&T LIA system building plan

 Concept and Significance of Legislative Impact Assessment
 Definition: An evidence-based legislative approach systematically reviewing law effects,
evaluating effectiveness and equity, and feeding results back into legislation
 Significance
- Achieves appropriateness, efficiency, and fairness in legislation
- Provides evidence base for legislative necessity and policy choices
- Enables dynamic norm formation: ex-ante ↔ ex-post evaluation cycle → continuous
learning and improvement

 Domestic and International LIA Status
 Korea(Executive): sector-specific ex-ante assessments (regulation, technology, gender,
employment, environment)
- Fragmented by ministry and law → limited integration and post-utilization
 Korea(National Assembly): expert review reports, cost estimation, pilot LIA projects
 International Cases
- EU: integrated economic·social·environmental assessment / “Better Regulation”
package → standardized items
- US: cost-benefit RIA for significant regulations / independent oversight
- Switzerland: constitutional basis → effectiveness·efficiency·centered evaluation
- Germany: GGO-based 3-phase cycle (ex-ante → concurrent → ex-post)
- Japan: ministry self-evaluation → coordination → public disclosure for feedback
 Key Implications
- Common elements across countries: problem definition → alternative comparison →
effect/side-effect analysis → cost-benefit review

 Framework for S&T-Specific LIA System
 Basic Direction
- S&T legislation → shift to dynamic normative framework premised on learning and
adjustment
- Full-cycle LIA: ex-ante → implementation → ex-post → learning & feedback
- Assessment scope: beyond cost estimation → innovation direction / social
acceptability / risk-benefit distribution / long-term transition effects

 Recommendations

 Integrate enforcement outcomes, post-LIA data, government assessment results
 Introduce forecasting, scenario analysis, early warning functions → proactive response
to high-uncertainty legislation
 Leverage AI → impact prediction / pattern analysis / stakeholder response detection

Recommendation 1. Data-driven analytical infrastructure with future forecasting
and scenario analysis

Recommendation 2. Institutionalize LIA with ex-ante ↔ ex-post linkage and
feedback mechanism
