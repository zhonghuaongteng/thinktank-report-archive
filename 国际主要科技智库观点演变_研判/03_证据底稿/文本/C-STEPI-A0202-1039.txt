6. 투명성(transparency)과 진실성(integrity)의 강화

Summary
Investigation on Strategic Direction for Public Procurement Centering on the
National Innovation and Security
ㆍProject Leader: Jonghwa Choi
ㆍParticipants: Yongrae Cho･ KIM KYU TAE･ KIM BYOUNG GUN･ Kwon,
Namho･ EunA Kim･ Hyun Jung Seo
The public procurement for innovation policy (PPI) has been put into effect
from July 2019. Despite the establishment of major policies and systems, there
has still been an increasing need of strengthening the competitive performance of
national innovation. The PPI in the aerospace sector driving future innovation, for
example, is especially in need of advancement, whereas a satisfactory track record
can be found such as the award of innovative prototype products - e.g., 994 items
in 2021 (Public Procurement Service, 2022).
On another level, a demand for government's more proactive procurement
capabilities has also been rising due to the recent transformative environment
such as an increasing volatility of global supply chain triggered by Covid-19.
Hence, it can be acknowledged that there is an urgent need of implementing a
more effective national procurement system through the recognition of the
criticality of supplying strategic commodities as the core element of national
security.
In light of this, a PPI policy needs to be understood as a core government
initiative to achieve successful national growth and innovation. Moreover, such
policy is also necessary to address and react to recent stagnation of growth and
technology/security threats.
The aim of study is to examine a proactive and strategic procurement policy
for explicitly addressing national threats to secure future growth engines. This
study also focuses on identifying key agenda to strategize current PPI policies
within the perspective of 'strategic procurement'.

In order to implement a strategic procurement policy, it is critical to set a clear
agenda from the viewpoints of both innovation demands and survival demands to
contribute to national policies. In other words, we argue that a strategic
procurement policy is the fundamental aim of next generation procurement policy
by introducing the concept of 'strategic procurement' to explore the future
development areas of Public Procurement Service. Particularly, the key results
include the implementation plan for a strategic procurement policy in four
different perspectives: 1) future demand, 2) national threats, 3) private finance
and 4) R&D.
This study contributes to our better understanding of PPI policy which provides
a solid foundation broadening its scope covering innovation, growth, security and
survival, beyond existing public market policies established by the government.
