Executive Summary

The main objective of the K-Innovation ODA Programme is to buil d and enhance the
STI policy capacities of each partner country, ranging from des ign and implementation,
to evaluation and feedback. The K - I n n o v a t i o n O D A P r o g r a m m e ’ s w ealth of
professional and academic experie nce has enabled the Kingdom of Cambodia
(Cambodia) project to produce important national strategies and technology roadmaps.
T h i s i s t h e f i r s t y e a r o f t h e P h a s e 3 p r o j e c t o f t h e K - I n n o v a t io n P r o g r a m m e w i t h t h e
Ministry of Industry, Science, Te chnology & Innovation (MISTI) o f t h e R o y a l
Government of Cambodia (RGC). The Phase 1 project, conducted fr om 2018 to 2019,
established Cambodia’s first national plan on science and techn ology and resulted in
the launch of “STI Policy 2020-2030” as the first official STI M a s t e r P l a n o f t h e R G C .
Subsequently, the Phase 2 project (2020 to 2022) developed six national technology
roadmaps (Education, Health, Agriculture, Digital, Energy, and Tourism) related to the
implementation of the National STI Master Plan. Despite the COVID-19 restrictions, the
project was managed and conducted effectively thanks to the joint efforts of STEPI and
MISTI. The Phase 3 project, which will be carried out from 2023 to 2025, is focused on
the establishment of a national R&D management system in Cambodia.
The consultation process for the project in 2023 consisted of the following main activities:
As a first step, STEPI and MISTI held a kick-off meeting on 7 F ebruary 2023 and agreed
on the key activities and sche dule of the 2023 programme. Then, STEPI participated in
Cambodia’s first “National STI Day” event held on 24-31 March 2 023. During this event,
STEPI and MISTI held a kick-off workshop for the third phase of the programme and a
launch ceremony for the three TR Ms, which were endorsed during the opening
ceremony by H.E. PM Mr. Hun Sen. He underlined the importance o f the TRMs as a
compass for Cambodia to plan and develop a strategy for acquiri ng the right

1. Introduction
This K-Innovation Partnership Programme with Cambodia is curren tly in the first year
of its third phase (2023-2025), titled “Project Consultation an d Pilot Implementation
for the Development of the National R&D Management System and t he R&D Centre of
Excellence in Cambodia”. The partner organisation is the Minist ry of Industry, Science,
Technology & Innovation (MISTI).
During the first phase of the K-Innovation Programme (2018 to 2 019), STEPI provided
policy consulting on STI policy governance and National STI law and supported the
Cambodian government in developi ng its first National STI Maste r Plan and National
STI Policy 2030-2030. In the se cond phase (2020 to 2022), the S TEPI team collaborated
w i t h C a m b o d i a n s t a k e h o l d e r s t o d e v e l o p a n a t i o n a l t e c h n o l o g y r oadmap (TRMs) for
each of six major technology are as, namely, education, health, agriculture, digital,
energy, and tourism. Three techno logy roadmaps (education, heal th, and agriculture)
were adopted by Cambodia’s National Council on Science, Technol ogy & Innovation in
July 2021, while the other three technology roadmaps were launc hed on Cambodia’s
first “National STI Day” in March 2023. As part of the second p hase of the K-Innovation
Programme with Cambodia, equivalent to an intermediate “Nationa l Innovation
System (NIS)” stage, the activities included selecting strategi c technologies and
industries, developing technology roadmaps, and improving capac ity building for STI
policy. The third phase of the programme is also a part of the intermediate NIS stage,

2. Objectives

The overall objectives of the th ird phase of K-Innovation Partn ership Programme with
Cambodia are to provide policy c onsulting aimed at advancing th e National R&D
Management System in Cambodia in order that its STI Master Plan and TRMs can be
implemented fully, and at building the skills and capacities of the researchers and the
related policymakers. In 2023, t he programme focused on conduct ing a survey on the
status of national R&D management and analysing the results. Re garding the topic of
national R&D management, Korean experts from KISTI shared Korea ’s rich experience
in developing and operating two national R&D management program mes, NTIS and
COMPAS, whose roles and functions are explained in details in C hapters 2 and 3
respectively. For the next two years, 2024 to 2025, STEPI’s res earch team will support
and assist MISTI in designing the conceptual and implementation plan for the national
R&D management system, which will be tailored to Cambodia’s nee ds and its national
innovation system.

steadily during this period, as shown in Figure 2-1. With the i ncrease of the national
R&D budget, the scale and complexi ty of national R&D projects a lso tended to grow

1.3 NTIS Service

The NTIS is the world's first national R&D information and know ledge portal to
integrate and provide comprehens ive R&D information including p rogrammes,
projects, outcomes, and human resources. It also provides 11.5 million datasets (as of
August 2023), including science a nd technology information such as academic papers,
patents, research reports, and policies. These data boast the b est quality among all
public institutes. The purpose o f t h i s s e r v i c e i s t o e n h a n c e t he efficiency of R&D
investment and the productivity o f research by collecting and s haring information on
national R&D, which until its establishment had been separately managed by diverse
ministries and institutes. The NTIS supports evidence-based R&D planning by providing
reference data for policymaking, provides transparent informati on on evaluations of
the performance of national R&D programmes and institutes, and prevents duplicated
expenditure on national R&D budgets.
Furthermore, it provides recommendations of user-customised inf ormation, supports
the management of R&D outcomes, and provides information on the current status of
national R&D, as well as providing customised information packa ges and information
on the latest research trends and the real-time analytical envi ronment of national R&D.

1.4 History
Every three years, the National Technology Information Service (NTIS) develops a new
plan through Information Strategy Planning (ISP) to set the fut ure direction for its
operations. Over time, these plans have led to significant chan ges in the NTIS's focus,
the types of information it provides, and its R&D support proce sses. Initially, the NTIS
was designed to assist project m anagers and policymakers. However, it has evolved to
support a broader audience, including all types of researchers. Moreover, the NTIS has
broadened its information servic es to include a diverse range o f data on national and
international R&D, policy trends , and science and technology de velopments. Today,
the NTIS is committed to enhanc ing its offerings to support the entire R&D lifecycle,
moving beyond its earlier focus on just segments of the R&D process.

4. Conclusion

4.1 Recommendations for Cambodia
Cambodia needs to introduce more systematic R&D management in t o maximise
research performance within a very limited R&D investment envir onment. As the size
of the R&D input budget is very small and the promotion of R&D at the national level is
important, it is necessary to monitor how efficiently the small -scale R&D budget is
invested. Accordingly, it is necessary to establish and operate an integrated national
R&D management system at the national level.
In the case of Costa Rica, national R&D projects are executed o n a smaller scale than
those of South Korea. As such, the system for carrying out and managing national R&D
projects was standardised and integrated into universities that were already operating
well. In the case of Cambodia, some universities, including the Cambodian Agricultural
University (RUA), were operating an internal project management system. In addition,
it seems positive that most of the organisations interviewed agreed on the necessity of
establishing an integrated project and outcome management syste m a t t h e n a t i o n a l
level.
Therefore, it is strongly reco mmended to conduct pre-feasibilit y studies, including
environmental analyses, before constructing the system. It is a lso advisable to develop
a pilot system, regardless of its size, and to allow relevant o rganizations to utilize it.
This approach not only demonstrates the system’s potential but also supports the case
for explaining its necessity and securing a budget for further development. To facilitate
this, securing funding in advance for the promotion and impleme ntation of the system
based on the results of the pre-feasibility study is crucial. It is recommended to
conduct the pre-feasibility study according to the following process :
• Analyse the Status of Cambodia’s National R&D Management System.
• Establish a target system and im plementation plan for Cambodia’ s national R&D
information management.

1.1 Background to the development of COMPAS
The exponential increase in global digital information calls fo r the development of
advanced technologies designed to manage, analyze, and effectiv ely utilize these
massive data volumes. These techn ologies are essential not just f o r c r a f t i n g t i m e l y
policies and making informed decisions but also for protecting research and fostering
advancements in the fast-paced realms of science and technology.
Both small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and individual r esearchers often
encounter difficulties in accessing substantial data or advance d data processing
technologies, making support for these technologies crucial for them. In light of this,
there is a growing interest in big data processing, and nationa l-level support is also on
the rise. COMPAS was conceived amidst this context, with the ai m of providing
systematic IT-based support from the government side to assist those who lack
information infrastructure and find themselves at a disadvantage.

1.2 Rationale
The Royal Government of Cambodia (RGC) has set forth an ambitio us vision to elevate
Cambodia to a high middle-income country by 2030 and a high-inc ome country by
2050. Central to achieving this vision is the enhancement of th e nation’s scientific and
technological capabilities and th e improvement of its innovatio n performance.
Strengthening these areas is crucial for promoting economic div ersification, boosting
economic dynamism, and fostering sustainable and inclusive deve lopment, thereby
contributing to national prosperity, peace, security, safety, a nd an improved quality of
life.
Advancing Cambodia’s scientific and technological expertise is essential for facilitating
the country’s transition from a labor-intensive to a knowledge- based economy. In line
with this vision, the RGC approved the National Science, Techno logy, and Innovation
(STI) Policy 2020-2030 in 2019. This policy leverages science, technology, research, and
development to support inclusive a nd sustainable development, g uided by a strategic
framework, objectives, and policies.
A key implementation example of this policy is the establishmen t of Cambodia's STI
Roadmap 2030. One of the roadmap’s pillars is to enhance resear ch capacity and
quality by strengthening the national higher education and research system to conduct

4.1 Concerns and Barriers
The implementation of a national research and development (R&D) management
system can encounter various con cerns and obstacles, as reveale d by insights from
researchers in the field. These challenges include technical is s u e s , d a t a p r i v a c y a n d
security concerns, lack of training, integration challenges, an d resistance to change, all
of which are clearly illustrated in Figure 4-26.

 Technical Challenges
Implementing a robust R&D manage ment system often involves usin g sophisticated
software, data analytics tools, a nd other technical solutions. The figure below shows
t h a t a s i g n i f i c a n t 6 5 . 6 7 % o f r e s pondents expressed concerns abo ut technical
challenges, emphasizing that adopting advanced tools and techno logies could impede
the seamless integration of the R &D system. These technologies can also be complex
to set up and maintain, requiring substantial investment in infrastructure and expertise.

 Data Privacy and Security Concerns
Research and development activities often involve handling sens itive and confidential
data, making data privacy and security paramount. Ensuring comp liance with data
protection regulations and safeguarding against breaches or una uthorized access are
significant challenges. The study shows that 61.19% of responde nts ranked data
privacy and security as the seco nd major concern, especially wh en handling sensitive
research data.

 Lack of Training
The introduction of a new R&D management system typically requi res comprehensive
training for the staff and researchers who will use the system. Inadequate training can
result in under-utilization, errors, and inefficiencies. About half of the respondents,
49.25%, expressed concerns about t he inadequacy of training for s t a f f , r e s e a r c h e r s ,

===== PDF_PAGE 155 =====

and public users, which could hinder the effective operation of t h e n a t i o n a l R & D
management system.

 Integration Challenges
 Researchers and users from different organizations, departments, and research teams,
including government, higher education, and private sector inst itutions, use their own
processes and systems. Integrati ng these diverse systems and pr ocesses into a unified
R&D management system is highly complex. Achieving seamless com munication and
data exchange between these comp onents is a significant challen ge. The findings
reveal that 49.25% of respondent s recognized integration challe nges as a substantial
barrier, given the need to harmonize diverse R&D processes and systems across
various organizations and departments.

 Resistance to Change
People and organizations often resist change, especially when i t involves departing
from established practices and adopting new ways of working. The results indicate that
25.37% of respondents considered resistance to change a signifi cant hurdle. They
expressed concern that individuals and institutions might hesit a t e t o e m b r a c e n e w
approaches and depart from establ ished practices, particularly when related to new
technology and systems.

To overcome these concerns and barriers, it is essential to est ablish a comprehensive
strategy for addressing each i ssue, promote awareness, and fost er collaboration
among all stakeholders in the R&D community. This strategy shou ld balance the need
for data sharing and collaboration with security measures and p rovide comprehensive
training programs, effective change management strategies, comm unication, and
leadership.
