Summary
A Study on linking regional R&D and the RISE system
: Focusing on linking the Ministry of Science and ICT’s regional projects with the
RISE system
ㆍProject Leader: Jieun Seong
ㆍParticipants: Hyeonchae Yang ․ Sunghwa You ․ Wichin Song ․ Eunyoung Kim
Regions are experiencing unprecedented crises and changes due to various
factors such as population decline and regional deterioration. In order to enhance
the survival and sustainability of regions, there is a need for new regional
innovation policies and R&D strategies that transcend existing frameworks, such
as top-down policy initiatives led by the central government and industry
development strategies centered on economic value. This study aimed to propose
connection and cooperation measures between the Ministry of Education’s RISE
system and the Ministry of Science and ICT’s regional innovation R&D projects,
in order to establish a region-led innovation ecosystem and enhance the local
embedding of innovative activities. The theoretical discussions and implications
regarding region-led integrated innovation were derived, and in-depth interviews
were conducted with stakeholders involved in the RISE project to explore its
operation. Additionally, the current status of Daejeon and the Ministry of
Science’s regional innovation projects were examined as representative cases of
the RISE initiative. Based on this, policy and institutional measures for linking
regional innovation R&D with the RISE project were proposed.
Currently, the RISE project is being promoted in 17 metropolitan cities, with
areas related to collaborative cooperation with research institutions and the operation
of regionally specialized research institutes being particularly notable in Daejeon,
Jeollanam-do, and Chungcheongnam-do. In-depth interviews with relevant
stakeholders confirmed the potential of transformative regional innovation policies
as niche experiments, but it was also found that there were aspects that could not

II A Study on linking regional R&D and the RISE system

escape from the path dependency and inertia of existing regional innovation policies.
The focus remains skewed towards industrial development rather than resolving
regional challenges, and there is still insufficient differentiation among regions. It
was noted that there is a lack of active participation from local residents or civil
society, which is far from the ‘quadruple helix’ model that emphasizes interaction
between universities, research institutions, industry, government, and civil society.
Based on this analysis, the study proposes the following policy tasks for
linking and cooperating the RISE system with local R&D.
First, policy integration is necessary for locally-led integrated regional innovation.
It is important to establish a vision from the perspective of regional transformation
and transformative regional innovation, overcoming the limitations of the lack of
strategic direction in ongoing regional innovation projects and the fragmented and
department-specific promotion patterns.
Second, in order for the RISE project to become a community innovation initiative,
both the content and structure must undergo a transformation towards a ‘regionally
innovation-centered paradigm.’ The goal of the RISE project is to “drive innovation
in the community and industry centered around universities;” however, many
universities remain focused on “university-centered,” “project-centered,” and
“outcome-centered” approaches, which diverges from the original purpose of
community innovation.
Third, it is necessary to establish problem-solving cooperation based on
community needs and co-creation, rather than unilaterally pushing out R&D and
technologies traditionally led by universities and research institutes. For this, it is
essential to build an integrated R&D system that combines Pre-R&D for
pre-planning (problem discovery and planning), R&D, and Post-R&D for
application and dissemination.
 Fourth, improvements in governance and legal systems are needed for linkage
and cooperation in R&D. For the cooperation between the Ministry of Education
and the Ministry of Science and ICT to be promoted, it is necessary to adjust
the relevant legal frameworks between research support based on general accounts
of R&D and research-education support utilizing government subsidies.
 Fifth, a cooperative system among local governments, universities, and research
