Executive Summary

The Vietnamese government has emphasized science, technology, and innovation (STI)
as the most important keys for implementing the SocioEconomic Development Strategy
(SEDS) 2011-2020 plan and realizing sustainable growth. Vietnam has relatively good STI
capacity and human resources among ASEAN countries. Unfortunately, however,
Vietnam’s STI capacity is still lower than that of other developed countries. In this regard,
the Vietnamese government needs a strategic approach to STI policies based on
scientific methodologies such as technology foresight, given the limited resources and
capacity.

Based on the confirmation and acceptance of the project proposal submitted by NISTPASS
(currently VISTI), the Korean government affirmed the support for Vietnam in making faster
progress toward achieving Vietnam’s STI development goals through the K-Innovation ODA
program “Policy Consultation on Strengthening the Capacity of Foresight for Science,
Technology, and Innovation (STI) Strategic Planning in Vietnam 2019-2020."
The Project aims to strengthen the capacity of key agencies and institutions of the Ministry
of Science and Technology in the strategic planning for Vietnam’s STI. The scope of the
Project has been defined based on Vietnam’s STI policy priorities and the lessons learned
from the Korean experiences in national STI policies and technology foresight. Accordingly,
the Project framework includes the following: i) review of Vietnam’s national STI strategies
and technology foresight experiences; ii) research on the Korean experiences in technology
foresight and national STI strategies; iii) local workshops for the diagnosis of Vietnam’s
technology foresight capacities and for the consensus on the policy consultation’s direction
and contents; iv) capacity building workshop with technical training programs to enhance
the technology foresight capacity of Vietnamese government officials and experts; and v)
development of the implementation plan for technology foresight by VISTI and review
process by STEPI and Korean experts.

At the kick-off meeting with VISTI, the objectives and scope of the Project were
discussed. STEPI and VISTI discussed f urther specific action plans and agreed on the
project programs and activities. During the workshop on national future strategy and
technology foresight, the researchers of bot h counterparts shared insights on Korea’s
S&T strategic planning and its implications on Vietnam’s capacity for national STI policy
and technology foresight. Based on the lessons learned from the workshop and the
consensus on the Project activities, VISTI and Vietnamese experts prepare the draft
framework of the pilot exercise, which will be specified and enriched through the
capacity building workshop.

The research team suggested implications and recommendations on how to develop
Vietnam’s capacity for national STI strategy and technology foresight . Regarding the
national STI strategy, it is necessary to make the government establish and implement
an STI strategy systematically with active commitment to making STI contribute to
national socioeconomic development. Vietnam needs to promote strategic investments
and systematic management to achieve the best possible outcomes given the limited
R&D resources. Vietnam also needs to improve its R&D structure to increase the
performance of the R&D system considering the limited R&D resources. For this, the role
of R&D organizations such as VAST, VISTI, and VKIST should be clearly identified.

Regarding the technology foresight, Vietnam lacks experts in technology foresight and has
a very weak system of gov ernment cooperation. There is also a need to develop a
technology foresight methodology suitable for the Vietnamese context. The most important
factor for the successful implementation and use of technology foresight is recognizing the
importance of STI and technology foresight in the political decision-making process and
cooperation of all the relevant government ministries.
Vietnam should explore its own growth path through the technology foresight. In this regard,
it is important to develop Vietnam's own technology foresight methodology by applying the
implications from the Korean experience to the Vietnamese environment and
characteristics.

1. Introduction
The Vietnamese government plans to become one of the industrialized countries by
establishing “Socioeconomic Development Strategy (SEDS) 2011-2020” aiming at political
and social stability, improvement of quality of life, and improvement of international status.
The Vietnamese government has historically emphasized the importance of science and
technology. In particular, science, technology, and innovation are recognized as the most
important keys to implementing the SEDS 2011-2020 plan and realizing sustainable growth.
In this regard, Vietnam has promoted science, technology , and innovation (STI)
development in order to achieve sustainable socioeconomic development goals.
For the transition to the industrialized economy, technological development is the most
important policy. In addition, to achieve the goal of increasing the proportion of hi-tech
industries to 45%, S&T capacity for industries such as electronics, ICT, and advanced
machinery should be developed.
Vietnam has relatively good STI capacity and human resources among ASEAN countries.
Unfortunately, Vietnam’s STI capacity is still lower than that of other developed countries.
According to the report of the World Economic Forum, Vietnam ranks 92nd and 73rd in the
areas of technology acceptance and innovation, respectively.
In Vietnam, where STI capacity and R&D investment are not sufficient to lead economic and
social development, there is a need for a strategic approach to promoting STI policies.
Vietnam’s R&D investment as a percentage of GDP is less than 0.5% (in 2013). T his is

and concentration strategy.” In other words, a national technology strategy is needed, and

Based on the confirmation and acceptance of the project proposal submitted by NISTPASS

development strategies and development needs as well as Korea’s comparative advantage.
Accordingly, the scope of the Project shall be based on the consideration of Vietnam’s STI
policy priorities and the lessons learned from the previous Korean experiences in national
STI policies and technology foresight.
The aim of the Project is to strengthen the capacity of key agencies and institutions of the
Ministry of Science and Technology in the mid- and long-term strategic planning of STI in
Vietnam.
The Science and Technology Policy Institute (STEPI) is a Korean government-sponsored
research institute and a leading global think tank that conducts research and analysis on
issues pertaining to science, technology, and innovation. STEPI works closely with various
ministries, regional governments, and relevant institutes to support policy design. It also has
a long experience in providing STI policy consultancy for developing countries, having the
role of conducting STI development cooperation activities with partner countries.

5. Project Activities
5.1 Activity 1: Preparation of the Project
From January to March 2019, the STEPI team conducts a literature review related to the STI
strategic planning and technology foresight of Vietnam. Any relevant documents on the

the kick-off meeting and first workshop will be developed based on the Project Concept

shared with VISTI for coordination.

The STEPI team visits Hanoi, Vietnam on April 15 to 19, 2019 for the kick-off meeting and
the first workshop. Specifically, the workshop aims to share Korea’s experiences in the
identified topics, including national strategic planning for STI, introduction of technology
foresight, and strategic planning of Government Research Institutes. The Vietnamese
counterpart will give a presentation on the STI Policy of Vietnam and technology foresight
in Vietnam in order to promote in-depth understanding of Vietnam’s current status and
experiences in foresight. Afterward, STEPI and VISTI will build consensus on the expected
results and outcome.

The capacity-building workshop will be held in October 2019 to share the best practices of
Korea and to practice technology foresight as an implementation strategy of the STI Policy.
VISTI shall invite a delegation of ten (more or less) national experts on the STI policy, and
STEPI shall manage the workshop activities. STEPI will provide guidelines of the capacity-
building workshop for one week. For the pilot -scale exercise of a Strategic Plan on
Technology Foresight, VISTI shall deliver its interest in key areas during the workshop.

1. Vietnam’s STI Status
1.1 Overview1
Vietnam has had a remarkable development record over the past 30 years . Economic
and political reforms under Đ ổi Mới, launched in 1986, have spurred rapid economic
growth and development and transformed Vietnam from one of the world’s poorest
nations to a lower middle-income country.
Vietnam’s economy continues to show fundamental strength , supported by robust
domestic demand and export -oriented manufacturing. The extreme poverty rate is
estimated to have declined to below 3 percent. Following the 6.8 percent growth in 2017,
preliminary data indicate that GDP growth soared to 7.1 percent in 2018, underpinned
by a broad-based pickup in economic activity.
Vietnam’s medium -term outlook is broadly favorable , and downside risks are tied to
weak external demand, shifting trade patterns, global financial volatility, and incomplete
banking and state-owned enterprise (SOE) reforms. On the upside, Vietnam is strongly
positioned to benefit from numerous free trade agreements that are coming into force
now and over the forecast period.
Vietnam’s growth is projected to be a moderate 6.6 percent in 2019 , driven by credit
tightening, slower private consumption , and weaker external demand. Inflationary

1.2 Science and Technology Policy2
Vietnam’s S&T and innovation policy objectives are still formulated in the context of a
planning approach to defining broader socioeconomic goals.
Aligned with the “Strategy for accelerating industrialization and modernization with a
socialist orientation for basically becoming an industrialized country by 2020” (2001-10) and
“National Socioeconomic Development Plan 2016 -2020,” The Vietnamese government
announced the “Science and Technology Development Strategy for 2011-20.”
“Science and Technology Development Strategy for 2011-20”
In April 2012, the Prime Minister of Vietnam approved the Sci ence and Technology
Development Strategy for 2011-20. The general goals of the Socioeconomic Development
Strategy (2011-2020) are to become a modern industrial country by 2020 and stabilize the
political community, improve people’s lives, protect the country’s sovereignty, and achieve
advanced status in the global market. The goals and main targets are as follows:
Goals
• To harmonize humanities and social sciences, natural science , and technological
development and make science and technology a driving force for the development of
a modern industrial society.
• To reach ASEAN/global level in some areas by 2020.

Targets
• To raise the value of high-technology and applied science products to about 45% of the
GDP by 2020 and ensure 15-17% annual growth in the science and technology market.

1.3.1 R&D expenditure (and budget for S&T)
(In terms of R&D Expenditure) According to a survey conducted by the Vietnamese
government in 2012, the total R&D expenditures in Vietnam in 2011 stood at 5,294 billion
dong (about 26.5 billion yen), and the ratio against GDP was 0.21%. This is considerably low
compared to that of Japan, lower than that of Singapore and Malaysia, at the same level as
that of Thailand, and higher than that of Indonesia and the Philippines among ASEAN
countries. It has not changed much from the 2002 figure (2002: 0.19% Source: UNESCO
SCIENCE REPORT 2010).
(Note) With regard to the total research and development expenditures in Vietnam, science
and technology statistics have previously been inadequate.
In 2012, a statistical survey was conducted in compliance with the OECD method under the
direction of the prime minister. The results are published in “Science and Technology in

(Mechanism Analysis) STI activities in Vietnam depend heavily on budget support, which
represents approximately 65-70% of the total national expenditure on S&T. Public
expenditure on S&T grew from approx. 0.2% of GDP in 1995 to around 0.5-0.6% in 2012.
More than two-thirds goes to applied research and technological development, and the rest,
to basic research.
Even after adjusting for lower labor costs, the available data suggest that many projects are
below critical mass and can be justified at best as capacity-building trials. This points to the
need for the Vietnamese government to ensure the highest efficiency in the use of limited
budgetary means (concentration to ensure impact) while filling major gaps in the system
(comprehensiveness to increase systemic efficiency).
Another striking feature is that public expenditure on S&T is mostly channeled through
ministries, incurring significant management costs. Although the available data do not allow
estimating precisely the amount that ultimately benefits those undertaking research
activities, there are concerns that a considerable part of the S&T budget is absorbed by the
administrative parts of ministries and beneficiary organi zations. Widespread red tape
complicates and slows all administrative processes in Vietnam, an issue that is further
discussed below.
The main steering mechanisms in Vietnam’s STI system are direct c ontrol over R&D
organizations, conditional provision of public goods (information) and basic infrastructures,
financial support to R&D organizations and activities in the form of grants (expenditures)
and, albeit marginally, tax incentives (foregone revenu es) especially for FDI. Other
instruments such as open procurement policies, public-private partnerships (PPPs), and
other more demand-side instruments do not play any significant role at present.

1.4.2 Human resource gaps in S&T governing bodies (S&T managers)
Although NIS has the institutions and conditions needed to facilitate innovation, it is
fragmented and in need of improvement. The system remains weak, isolated, decentralized,
bureaucratic, uncoordinated, ad hoc, and lacking in clear responsibilities. It includes over
3,200 managers, each of whom takes part in formulating policies and procedures,
implementing S&T programs, and supervising staff.
Vietnam’s Ministry of Science and Technology (MoST) is the main ministry for coordinating
S&T, building capacity, guiding national research programs, and measuring the impact of
S&T. Ministries that are also involved in S&T include the Ministry of Industry and Trade
(MoIT), Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (MARD), and Ministry of Education
and Training (MoET). There are provincial Departments of Science and Technology (DoST)
offices in all 63 provinces and 1 ,100 GRIs reporting to various agencies at the central,
provincial, and university levels.

2. Technology Foresight in Korea

2.1 Introduction to Technology Foresight
2.1.1 Rationale of Technology Foresight14
Historically, mostly in developed countries when they were in the industrial development
stage, technology foresight has been the critical tool for STI policymaking. In the 1970s,
Japan institutionalized TF as a system for the analysis of opportunities given by new STI
development to aid in long-term STI policymaking (mostly Delphi Survey). In the 1980s and
especially the 1990s, a similar ambitious TF program was developed in Canada and several
European countries (in the face of globalization and rising competition) . International
organizations -- notably the EU, APEC (particularly influential in Southeast Asia), and UNIDO
(especially in Latin America and Eastern Europe) -- organized training programs, held
conferences, and produced guides to best practices. Noteworthy is the fact that the EU
required new members from Central and Eastern Europe to adopt and utilize Technology
Foresight in developing their own practices. In some countries, it was practiced as half-
hearted exercise, or “me too” fashion.
Note, however, that it is booming again due to increasing uncertainty and complexity.
Recently, there have been some emerging challenges in STI policies.
• Digital Technology Evolution (capitalism without capital)
• Difficulties in assimilating new generations of technology (generating & exploiting new
knowledge)
• Reconciling pressures on public finances with requests to fund new initiatives in STI
• Cumbersome and ineffectual structures of the existing governance of STI
• Social unease about some aspects of science and some applicat ions of technology
(notably biosciences and nuclear technology)

2.1.2 Characteristics of (Technology) Foresight15
The rationale of technology foresight as explained in 2.1.1 comes from the characteristics of
foresight.
• Acknowledgement of diverse possible futures
- The f uture is not predetermined but can develop diversely according to the
present efforts.
- The future is something that is uncertain, opening to be better.

• Action-oriented
- Foresight necessitates not only analyzing or thinking about future development
but also creating it.
- Thus, Foresight could be useful only when the results are translated into action.

• Participatory
- Foresight is conducted not by minor professionals but diverse stakeholders.
- The results of the TF are open to the public, which would then give feedback.

• Multi-disciplinary
- The target problems that are expected to be solved by foresight exercises are
actually not bound to one area or discipline.
- All the possibly related variables that may have indirect or direct impacts on the
issue should be considered through the multi-disciplinary approach.

These characteristics not only make foresight useful for policymaking but also suggest how
to frame the foresight tasks well. In other words, these characteristics are important since
they are often recalled for the basic principles and guidelines of technology foresight.

2.2 History of Korea’s Technology Foresight16
According to Korea’s Science and Technology Basic Law, Korea conducts technology
foresight every five years. Implemented by the Korea Institute of S&T E valuation and
Planning (KISTEP), the project runs for one and a half years. The Korean technology foresight
aims to provide the vision and direction of emerging S&T areas by identifying new
technologies that may have high potential in terms of growth of national wealth and
betterment of quality of human life.
Based on the technology foresight, Korea develops a new S&T Basic Plan every five years
alongside the launch of a new administration. All Science and Technology planning activities
at the national level are linked to the National Science and Technology Basic Plan. The
National Mid- and Long-term Science and Technology Development Strategy selects the
national core strategic technologies based on the TF results on the futu re technologies.
Based on this, the government establishes a strategic technology roadmap. The
technological development of the focused initiatives of the S&T Basic Plan reflects it. The
652 future technologies identified by the fourth TF went through a review process by
committees responsible for the national R&D budget as well as by R&D-related ministries.
As a result, a list of 120 national strategic technologies was compiled; these technologies
were also identified by the third S&T Basic Plan . The importance attributed to Korea’s
technology foresight (TFs) has grown with every round of TF (Choi M., Choi H., 2015).
Since the first TF for the period 1993–1994, Korean TF has been further developed and
advanced in response to the socioeconomic changes and to the increasing demands for TF
exercises. Since the Framework Act on Science and Technology in 2001, which specified
regularly carrying out TFs, national TFs have been conducted every five years. Thus, from
the third TF onward, Korea has been carrying out TFs every five years. In 2007, the third TF
was revised to increase complementarities with the S&T Basic Plan, the nation’s top-level
plan in the field of S&T. The results of the revised TF were directly reflected to the second
S&T Basic Plan. Furthermore, the results of the fourth TF (conducted during the period
2010–2011) found expression in the third S&T Basic Plan. All four TFs performed to date

2.4 Policy Implications
From the Korean case review, some major policy implications can be drawn. There are some
common features of technology foresight exercises that can be found in many other
countries’ cases aside from Korea’s case. At the same time, there are some policy lessons
that can be relatively more suitable for Vietnam. This section suggests the main implications
from those two aspects.
First of all, most of the Technology Foresight (TF) exercises have similar or common features.
• TF is d esigned to wield an influence on STI policy . Therefore, having access to the
decision makers or high-level officials who may influence on the decision making of STI
policy is essential. In many cases, the decision makers are the sponsors of the exercises.
The expected influence can be identifying priority areas for R & D or training.
• TF plays a role in addressing weaknesses in the innovation system, especially in the
process of aligning different stakeholders around shared appraisals of future prospects.
• Typical products will include: codified reports, presentations, Internet resources, etc.
• TF involves “process benefits” by connecting the dots among stakeholders and, equally
important, enabling key actors.
• TF is liable to be participatory and to establish networks among various sources of
knowledge of different regions and institutions.
• TF is p rospective by definition as it takes a longer -term view than the usual
policymaking does.

There are some other specific lessons for Vietnam and other countries that try to adopt or
develop their technology foresight capacity and policies:
• There is no one-size-fits-all methodology.
- Regard it as an essential point of TF exercises by considering the local conditions
and circumstances.

1.4.1 Concept note
a) Background
Technology development is much faster and broader than many people realize. It affects
every aspect of human life. Digital technology revolution such as artificial intelligence, big
data, Internet of Things, block chain technology, 5G, etc. even opens up a new possibility in
triggering industrial transformation. It may result in shifting the global order or solidifying
the current one. It is unclear at the moment who will be the next winner.
The future is shaped and influenced by the decisions made today. Still, making the right
decision at the right time to exploit the full potential of new technology and innovation
becomes more difficult and probably almost impossible. The time allowed for decision
making is getting shorter in contrast to the increasing degree of complexity and uncertainty
of issues. Though the volume of data is getting huge, insight by sense making is too shallow.
The only way to adapt and survive in the future is to embrace the complexity and

===== PDF_PAGE 96 =====
2019 K-Innovation ODA Program with Vietnam
88
uncertainty as the inherent characteristics of the system instead of trying to predict the
future. It is impossible to predict the future by forecasting through trend extrapolations.

b) Foresight capacity to shape the future
Foresight refers to approaches to informing decisions made robustly by improving inputs
concerning the longer-term future and by engaging the wider social stakeholders and linking
strategic vision to present-day actions. Foresight will enhance the capacity for decision
making and promote public debates by providing opportunities to discuss longer-term
trends as well as the expected outcome or implications of short-term decisions. Likewise,
foresight provides alternative interactive learning platforms for robust decision making in
the context of 4th industrial revolution-associated uncertainties.
c) Goal of the project
Nonetheless, realizing the importance of long -term thinking and the need for running
foresight exercise is different from the action on vision from the foresight results. In many
cases, foresight has limited influence on changing the behavior and action of the decision
makers due to many causes such as unclear goals, governance, incentives and cultures,
budgets, etc.
Since the lack of competence among decision makers turns out to be one of the main
reasons, the primary goal of the current project is set to enhance the foresight capacity of
the decision makers through the pilot-scale foresight exercise, focusing mainly on research
and innovation-driven future development. The opportunities provided by such innovations
intimately shape most areas of human social life.
Structured approaches are employed to build capacities in foresight, engagement, and
integration among the decision makers. The approaches will be interactive and participative,
focusing on long-term social, economic, and technological developments relevant to the
country. Based on the project’s goal, this workshop is designed to be the first step of the
project for sharing the current status of Vietnam’s national S&T policy and Korea’s
experiences in strategic planning and technology foresight. All the participants are invited
to discuss the agenda and its implications to Vietnam’s national STI strategy.

1. STI Policy and System
Vietnam's top national development plan is “Socioeconomic Development Strategy (SEDS)
2011-2020,” with “Socioeconomic Development Plan (SEDP) 2016-2020” established as an
implementation plan to realize this. The main objectives here are to stabilize the macro
economy and to increase economic growth, to restructure the economy and to change the
growth model, and to enhance productivity and competitiveness. Vietnam has traditionally
emphasized the importance of science and technology, and it considers science, technology,
and innovation (STI) to be the most important pillar in the implementation of socioeconomic
development strategies.
It is necessary to make the government establish and implement STI strategy systematically
with active commitment to making STI contribute to national socioeconomic development.
Note, however, that Vietnam does not have much experience in making and implementing
such STI policies linked with the national development plan. Vietnam lacks linkage between
national development goals – sectoral development goals – ministerial strategies, and
action plans. In particular, this linkage should be clear to strengthen the role of STI. In order
to establish the science and technology plan and strategy linked to national development
goals and to take a new leap in li ne with the new economic and social changes, the STI
system needs to be transformed. That means switching to a more creative, fast following
system.
This requires an upgrade of Vietnam's science and technology innovation system. First of all,
strategic investments and systematic management are needed to achieve the best possible
outcomes given limited R&D resources. This means a simultaneous policy of the so-called

===== PDF_PAGE 118 =====
2019 K-Innovation ODA Program with Vietnam
110
second-generation R&D and third-generation R&D in the research and development stage.
The third-generation R&D system involves mapping the national development goals and
science and technology goals and strengthening strategic linkages, whereas the second-
generation R&D system includes systematically managing R&D activities through R&D
evaluation, project management, and performance management. To this end, it should be
reformed into evidence-based or data-based science and technology policy, which requires
the establishment of a system for collecting, managing, and utilizing related data.
On the other hand, it is also necessary to consider improving the R&D structure to increase
the performance of the R&D system given limited R&D resources. The role of R&D
organizations such as VAST, VISTI, and VKIST, which ar e government or public research
institutes, should be established. In addition, a linkage system with universities and
industries needs to be strengthened for these public research institutes to create R&D
synergy effects.

2. Technology Foresight
Technology foresight involves predicting and analyzing future technologies in all areas of
science and technology, taking into account the changes in the internal and external
environments. As suggested above, this is an important tool to link national development
goals with science, technology, and innovation. In establishing and implementing a national
socioeconomic development strategy, Vietnam can use the results of technology foresight
to lay the groundwork for more strategic investments and policies. The national STI strategy
should be linked to other sectors such as economy, society, and education according to the
needs of the state, based on the results of technology foresight.
Vietnam has policy experience in performing technology forecasting or foresight, but such
can hardly be seen as successful. One reason for this is the lack of experts in technology
foresight and a very weak system of government cooperation. In addition, it is not possible
to develop a tec hnology foresight methodology suitable for the Vietnamese context by
using the methodology of foreign countries without internalizing. It is important to develop
Vietnam's own technology foresight methodology by applying the implications from the
Korean experience to the Vietnamese environment and characteristics.
Using the results of technology foresights in STI policies and national development
strategies requires clarifying the purpose at the planning stage. In recent years, the
international community, including Korea, has placed the focus of technology foresight on
the creation of innovation and the development of the entire economic society rather than
science and technology development. Likewise, technology foresight is oriented toward a
whole-of-the-government approach among ministries. In recent years, uncertainties have
been increasing in society, such as politics, economy, and technology, all over the world,
and the existing prediction methodology is confirmed not to be applied anymore. The new
paradigm and necessity of foresight, rather than prediction, are highlighted anew. Therefore,
technology forecasting and foresight are a process of finding direction, not content, with
content to be filled afterward. In particular, Vietnam should explore its own growth path
through technology foresight.

===== PDF_PAGE 120 =====
2019 K-Innovation ODA Program with Vietnam
112
The roles and objectives of technology foresight also vary according to the stage of
technology development. The role of scientists and engineers is important during the
following phase, whereas the innovation ecosystem, or environmental infrastructure, is
more important at the innovation phase. For the countries in the following phase, such as
Vietnam, the search for technology solutions to current problems should be key elements
of technology foresight.
Technology foresight can give the direction for developing countries like Vietnam to foster
their core industries in the age of the Fourth Industrial Revolution. In particular, there is a
high probability of success when appropriate and prompt support is provided through
selection and concentration using limited science and technology resources. Based on the
selection and concentration strategies, the STI policy needs several activities to be
conducted with regard to technology demand surveys, technology forecasting, roadmaps,
and analysis of STI capabilities.
The most important thing for the successful implementation and use of technology foresight
is recognizing the importance of STI and technology foresight in the political decision -
making process and cooperation of all the relevant government ministries. Extensive policy
efforts are also needed to reconcile and coordinate the demands and interests of not only
the government but also the people and social communities.
The STI plan and policy should be implemented in line with the national “Socioeconomic
Development Strategy (SEDS) 2011-2020” and “Socioeconomic Development Plan (SEDP)
2016-2020.” For example, the government set the national future strategies based on future
study. Technology foresight is followed to show the direction of the national STI master plan.
The STI plan includes the vision, goals, and strategies of STI development as well as the key
technologies needed to achieve the national development goals. For the next step, it needs
a national technology roadmap for these key technologies. Finally, each ministry or
institution in charge of each sector establish es and executes a strategy that includes a
technology roadmap for each sector.
