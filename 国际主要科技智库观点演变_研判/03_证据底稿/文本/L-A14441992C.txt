 28 Years of Support for SMEs Technology Innovation Promotion, Growing to
a Similar Scale as SBIR
 The KOSBIR program, introduced in 1998 to promote SME technology development,
has expanded twelvefold to ₩4.16 trillion (as of 2024), a scale comparable to that of
the U.S. SBIR program.
 KOSBIR supports a much higher volume of projects across all industries
(manufacturing-focused), SBIR evaluation is impact -driven(startup ratio, regional
balance, job creatio n), whereas KOSBIR relies on quantitative indicators(sales,
employment, R&D investment).

 Significant sales and employment effects through KOSBIR support,
requiring monitoring of fundamental performance indicators
 Analysis shows KOSBIR support has a positive quantitative effect on SMEs, leading to
higher growth rates in sales, employment, wages, and R&D investment compared to
unsupported firms.

 KOSBIR-generated patents are quantitatively high but low in quality
compared to SBIR
 KOSBIR patents are quantitatively high(28.1% of national R&D patents), significantly
more than SBIR(1.53%).
 The K-PEG evaluation of Korean registered patents shows a low proportion of excellent
patents and a high proportion of inadequate patents, with B1 (Normal) ranking at
33.4%

 Direction of the SME Technology Innovation Support System
 Transition from quantity-centric to quality-centric support, emphasizing innovation
and challenge by applying Government Entrepreneurship.
 Strengthen KOSBIR governance implementation and establish a data platform.

 Establish a consistent, long-term policy effectiveness analysis system by integrat ing
environmental variables and advanced methodologies.
 Develop unified performance indicators across ministries and shift evaluations from
short-term outputs to medium- and long-term market value.

 To enhance KOSBIR efficiency, clarify eligibility criteria and disclosure requirements
while strengthening conditions for projects with low SME participation
