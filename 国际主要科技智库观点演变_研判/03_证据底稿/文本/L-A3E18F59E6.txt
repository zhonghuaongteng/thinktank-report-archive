Issues and Implications on the CETs Information Protection in Domestic
and Overseas Laws of Emerging Security Fields
Yongrae CHO· Sung-A KIM· Sunyub KIM

 Countries are strengthening their laws, systems, and information security
policies to prepare for and respond to emerging security risks in addition
to traditional security concerns like defense and diplomacy
 Major countries are treating emerging security issues, such as economic sec urity, as
core strategic areas of national security and are strengthening laws and systems to
respond proactively; this is expected to have a direct impact on South Korea as well.
 There is a growing trend where not only diplomatic adversaries but also allied nations
are strengthening penalties without exception for the leakage of their own military and
non-military secrets, and expanding the scope of state secrets.
 As the scope of national security expands to include emerging security domains, the
scope of information protection under domestic and overseas laws is also expanding;
therefore, an analytical investigation of domestic and overseas situations is necessary
to formulate policy measures

 This paper sets two objectives based on the following conc erns
regarding information protection
 (Legal Survey) What are the domestic and international laws and policy examples
regarding the protection of state-critical information?
 (Typological Analysis) How can information security laws and policy examples b e
structured and categorized?
SUMMARY

===== PDF_PAGE 4 =====
 By conducting a comprehensive survey of information security provisions in domestic
and overseas laws related to emerging security issues and categorizing and structuring
their characteristics, this study aims to provide information on the latest policy trends
and offer policy recommendations based on the analysis

 Typological Analysis of Legal Provisions from an Information Security
Perspective with Subject to Korea, Japan, and China
 Information security issues in emerging security-related domestic and overseas laws
have been categorized and structured
 Laws by three countries of Korea, Japan, and China have been categorized into two
types: ‘Subjects of Information Protection’ and ‘Required Measures’
 (Subjects of Information Protection) The ‘Subjects of Information Protection’
category is defined as ‘Information Related to Critical Technologies’
 (Measures) The ‘Measures’ category is divided into ‘Prevention’ and ‘Incident
Response’ based on preparedness and response aspects
 Domestic and overseas laws have emphasized the context of non-traditional security;
overseas trends show a tendency to maintain the linkage between traditional and non-
traditional security through amendments to existing laws

 Rather than measures centered on justification and rationale-such as “establishing a
national control tower” or “enacting a unified law” -an approach is needed that
implements feasible practical measures while maintaining the current system, taking
into account the realities of South Korea’s policy landscape

Suggestion 1. Strengthen channels for exchange and linkage between
legislative and policy information, as well as a consistent enforcement system

 Given the current situation where multiple operational frameworks for information
and technology protection exist under different laws, t he exchange of policy
information and mutual monitoring among ministries are essential measures
 Practical measures to strengthen inter -legislative linkages and enhance the
functionality of enforcement system channels
- ① Implementation of follow -up practi cal measures and revision of laws and
regulations to strengthen the linkage between recent legislation related to emerging
security and existing legislation related to national security
- ② Strengthening the functions of a consistent enforcement system and related
channels to enhance the effectiveness and operational feasibility of current strategic
technology information protection measures

 (Categorization of Information Protection) It is necessary to c lassify and
systematically organize the concept and scope of “information,” as well as
preparedness and response measures, according to the objectives and purposes of
each law
 (Strengthening Related Measures) While reflecting the current era of emerging
security threats, it is necessary to develop policies that align with the philosophy and
objectives of each law, and to revise the legal framework by defining the ‘objects of the
act’ and ‘perpetrators’ more broadly

Suggestion 2. Substantive improvement of follow-up measures for information
protection preparedness and response based on comparative analysis and
categorization of legislation
