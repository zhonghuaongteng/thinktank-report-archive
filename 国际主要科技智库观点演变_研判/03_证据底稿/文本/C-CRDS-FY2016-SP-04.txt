This report presents a proposal for R & D strategy on fundamental technologies of
the low voltage distribution network and energy demand of households connected with
the network beyond 2050.
As of 2050, large volume of photovoltaic 㸝PV㸞 in homes is assumed to be penetrated,
and excess amount of power will be supplied to end-users. This means that consumers
in homes will play dual roles in both energy supply and consuming, that is to say, in
2050, the energy supply and demand structure in the low-voltage distribution network
will change drastically. Based on these circumstances, this report aims to explore and
propose technology to promote for the ideal situations of low voltage distribution
network as well as human behaviors on energy demand in homes.
In May 2016, the Plan for Global Warming Countermeasures was decided by the
Japanese Cabinet. The plan defines a path to achieve a mid-term target of 26%
greenhouse gas emission reduction by 2030 compared to 2013, and sets a long-term
goal to pursue 80% reduction by 2050. In order to accomplish 2050 long-term goal, it
must be important to decarbonize electric power supply ultimately. Large-scale
introduction of renewable energy, especially PV including household use is a promising
option.
