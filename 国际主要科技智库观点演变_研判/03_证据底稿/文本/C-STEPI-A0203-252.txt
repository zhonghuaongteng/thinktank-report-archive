1. Introduction

The Royal Government of Cambodia is striving to achieve its Rectangular Strategy Phase IV
through the 2019-2023 National Strategic Development Plan (NSDP) w i t h t h e m i d - a n d
long-term vision of becoming an Upper Middle-Income Country by 2030 and a High-
Income Country by 2050.
Driven by its long-term vision toward 2030, particularly as expressed in the Rectangular
Strategy III and IV, Cambodia has prepared and adopted the Camb odian Industrial
Development Policy 2015-2025 (IDP 2015-2025) as a guide to prom oting the country’s
industrial development that will help maintain sustainable and inclusive high economic
growth and reach Middle-Income economy status.
In this regard, Cambodia has pro moted science, technology, and innovation (STI)
development in order to achieve sustainable socioeconomic development goals. As a first
step, Cambodia has established the National Science and Technol ogy Council (NSTC),
which is assisted by a secretariat called the General Secretariat of the National Science and
Technology Council (GS-NSTC).
G S - N S T C a i m s , i n b r o a d t e r m s , t o c o n t r i b u t e t o t h e s u s t a i n a b l e socioeconomic
development of Cambodia through the formulation of the National STI plan, which will
guide domestic research and development (R&D) infrastructure bu ilding, capacity
expansion, and government research projects. It also has the mandate of improving the

2. Major Technology Roadmaps of Korea

2.1 National Critical Science and Technology Strategic Roadmap
2014-2023
The National Critical Science and Technology Strategic Roadmap was established as a pan-
ministerial strategic roadmap in 2014 in accordance with the “T hird Framework for the
Basic Plan on Science and Technology.” With Korea's entry into a n u l t r a - a g i n g s o c i e t y
becoming faster and the potentia l economic growth rate possibly dropping below 1
percent after 2030, it was necess ary to pre-respond to accelera tion in global S&T
development and rapid changes in the environment at home and abroad and to enhance
investment effectiveness by strategically planning R&D. Recognizing the importance of the
technology roadmap, the Korean government already established the NTRM in 2002 and
the Total Roadmap in 2007, but they have not been effectively utilized due to the lack of
regular modification and supplemen tation. The “Third Basic Plan of Science and
Technology” was established in 2013, and a new national technology roadmap was drawn
up accordingly for the technology areas selected as critical te chnologies among 120
national strategic technologies. S ta rt i ng w ith th e laun c hing of t h e s t e e r i n g c om m i t t e e i n
July 2013, the draft document was prepared in December, and the g o v e r n m e n t
announced it in April 2014 followi ng consultation with related ministries and public
hearings in January and March 2014.

techniques. Many techniques were discovered and involved to prepare emulsions such as

in people’s dietary patterns with many options of nutrient-poor , unbalanced, and
processed food and sugary beverages.
Childhood and adolescence diets not only influence the immediate health of children but

to support normal growth and deve lopment, and appropriate amount s o f m i n e r a l s a r e
required since deficient intake of certain minerals can cause diseases and lead to abnormal
development (ACC/SCN, 2000).

1. STI Policy
Cambodia has maintained stable economic growth of around 7% in recent years, and it is
pushing for a national strategy to develop into a high middle-income country by 2030 and
a high-income country by 2050. Note, however, that tourism and real estate development
are a major part of the economy, with the industrial sector focusing heavily on textiles and
clothing. To realize the national vision, it is necessary for C ambodia to find new growth
engines.

New growth engines can mostly be acquired through science and technological innovation.
It means that the key for the implementation of Cambodia's nati onal development
strategy is science and technological innovation. Cognizant of this background, STEPI has
provided policy advice and support in cooperation with GS-NSTC in Cambodia to establish
“STI Policy 2019-2025,” the first official Cambodian science and technology master plan.
The plan aims to secure science and technology personnel, expand science and technology
investment, strengthen the legal and institutional foundations, a n d f o s t e r a c l o s e
relationship between the social system and STI.
A particular area of emphasis in Cambodia's science and technol ogy policy is human
resource development (HRD), one of the highest-priority policy areas in the Rectangular
Strategy IV as the Cambodian government's national development strategy. In particular,
securing qualified scientists, engineers, and technicians in th e field of science and
technology is one of the most fundamental and critical success factors in the realization of
STI policy. That is why STEM education and related vocational t raining should be
emphasized. looking at the current situation in Cambodia, however, there is a shortage of
qualified teachers in science and technology, including lack of facilities and equipment for
science and technology education. Considering Korea's experienc e , i t i s r e c o m m e n d e d
that vocational education for the technicians and higher education system in science and
technology for researchers be developed in parallel, from the m id- and long-term
perspectives.

Upgrading Cambodia's STI system requires strengthening the governance of STI policy.
Cambodia currently does not have a central administrative body t h a t l e a d s a n d
coordinates STI policies. The National Science and Technology C ouncil (NSTC) and its
General Secretariat under the Ministry of Planning is now playi ng such role. Under this
governance structure, the importance of STI in policy prioritie s across the government
cannot be emphasized, and it is difficult to get sufficient support. Chaired by the Prime
Minister, NSTC is usually held twice a year, making it difficult to address the key agenda of
important STI policies in a timely manner. Therefore, it is recommended that the General

l a s t y e a r . T h r o u g h t h i s p r o c e s s , t h e o v e r a l l c o n c e p t o f t e c h n o logy roadmap is more

intelligence of participating experts. Through this, it is important to communicate with the
relevant community and form consensus. In that sense, there are still some activities that
are not well-organized.
Still, a significant achievement was that the pilot project was m a d e t o r e c o g n i z e t h e
importance of the technology roa dmap and to understand the basi c concepts and
procedures by science and techno logy policy organizations such a s G S - N S T C . I t i s a l s o
important to be able to cultivate experts who can play the role of leader or champion in
each sector by carrying out the national technology roadmap project in the second phase.
The role of the government is important for the successful development of the national
technology roadmap. Governments should not directly interfere w ith the roadmap
contents but facilitate and coordinate instead the roadmap process itself. And because the
technology roadmap reflects strategic priorities at the nationa l level, there is a high
likelihood of conflicting interes t s b e t w e e n t e c h n o l o g i e s . F o r this reason, governments
should always manage the process so that it is fair and transparent during the roadmap
p r o c e s s . A n o t h e r r o l e o f t h e g o vernment is to support sufficien t resources, such as
supporting staff and budget, for the technology roadmap. The go vernment should not
p r e s s u r e t h e r o a d m a p t e a m t o f i n i s h t h e r e p o r t t o o s o o n b u t s h ould allow time for
sufficient discussion and consensus. Finally, the end-user of the technology roadmap is the
company, so it is always necessary to encourage the experts fro m the private sector to
participate actively in the roadmap.
