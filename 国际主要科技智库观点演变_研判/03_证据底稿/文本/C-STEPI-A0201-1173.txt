The “Implementation of the Application Process and Capacity Building of the TC Start-up
and Incubation System in Azerbaijan” project represents a strategic initiative aimed at
strengthening Azerbaijan’s innovation ecosystem. This effort seeks to establish a robust
start-up and incubation framework support ed by comprehensive capacity -building
programs, thereby addressing critical gaps and positioning Azerbaijan as a regional hub for
innovation.
In recent years, Azerbaijan has made significant progress in diversifying its economy beyond
its traditional reliance on sectors such as oil and gas. The Technology and Creativity (TC)
sector has emerged as a key pillar of this diversification strategy, reflecting the country’s
aspiration to foster a dynamic and innovative economic environment. Despite its potential,
the TC sector faces challenges, including inadequate infrastructure, limited expertise, and
misaligned policy frameworks, which have hindered the growth of start-ups and incubation
systems. In response, this project was conceptualized to address these barriers and cultivate
a thriving entrepreneurial ecosystem in Azerbaijan.
The primary objectives of the project include enhancing the skills and expertise of key
stakeholders through targeted capacity-building initiatives and aligning institutional policies
to support sustainable innovation. These goals have been operationalized through a series
of carefully planned activities aimed at driving meaningful change across the ecosystem.
The project began by engaging stakeholders to understand the specific needs of Azerbaijani
start-ups. This engagement informed the design of a digital application platform, developed
to ensure transparency and accessibility. The platform was piloted with a select group of
start-ups to gather feedback and subsequently refined to improve its functionality.
Complementing this initiative, tailored capacity -building programs were introduced to

To address policy-related challenges, the project undertook a comprehensive review of

valuable lessons for future initiatives. Resistance to change from traditional institutions

adaptive approaches. These experiences highlight the importance of early stakeholder

sustainability and scalability of interventions.

 Project Background

According to the definition provided by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO),
technology transfer plays a pivotal role in facilitating the entire life cycle of a technology,
encompassing its inception, diffusion into the market, and eventual commercialization1.
Within this context, the commercialization of technologies entails a process in which an idea
transitions from a knowledge base to the market, thereby creati ng financial value.
Technology commercialization (TC) also enhances economic efficiency by offering
consumers a diverse and improved range of goods and services, resulting in the generation
of value-added revenue streams. Consequently, investment in technology and research and
development (R&D)-related activities, along with the subsequent commercialization of their
outcomes, necessitates the involvement of a wide array of institutions, programs, and
mechanisms to achieve the optimal results.
In the context of Azerbaijan, technology commercialization (TC) has recently emerged as a
prominent topic of discussion within its innovation ecosystem. This report is an integral part of
a collaborative project between the Azerbaijan High Technologies Park (HTP) and the Science
and Technology Policy Institute (STEPI) spanning 2022 to 2024. The report's primary focus is to
delve into the nuances of technology transfer and commercialization that are s pecific to
Azerbaijan. To accomplish this objective, our research will explicitly centre on the developments
occurring in Azerbaijan post-July 2022, coinciding with significant institutional reforms in the
aforementioned domains. We anticipate that this report will contribute significantly to
enhancing the effectiveness of the STEPI-HTP partnership's operational programs.

Consistent with the WIPO's delineation, effective technology transfer requires universities
and research institutions to operate within a well-structured innovation ecosystem2. This
ecosystem comprises an interconnected network involving governmental bodies, industry
stakeholders, research institutions, and various enabling factors, including human capital,
technology transfer infrastructure, and the sophistication of businesses and markets. Within
such an ecosystem, these entities pool their resources and expertise to collaboratively drive
innovation, ultimately serving regional and economic development objectives.
Recognizing these imperatives, Azerbaijan has established a comprehensive framework of
institutions and infrastructures to regulate and bolster TC activities. For instance, the
"Social-Economic Development Strategy of the Republic of Azerbaijan, 2022-2026" outlines
a strategic framework, part of which is dedicated to enhancing the competitiveness of the
country's human capital and positioning it as a hub for innovation. Consequently, Azerbaijan
aspires to create a "Science -Industry Cluster 4.0" concept -SABAH.city. SABAH.city is a
transformative innovation cluster slated for launch in 2026, driven by the Ministry of Science
and Education in collaboration with key public and private stakeholders. The initiative aims
to create a thriving ecosystem that fosters research, development, and innovation,
positioning SABAH.city as a major regional hub. The development is planned across three
strategic phases:
Phase 1: 2024-2026 - Laying the foundations and building initial traction
During this foundational phase, SABAH.city will begin establishing initial facilities across
approximately 4 hectares, setting the stage for future growth. Key components, including
startup incubators, accelerators, venture studios, venture capital funds, and technology
transfer offices, will be operational, providing essential support for emerging businesses. In
addition, leading public and private educational institutions and pioneering companies will
relocate and establish themselves as the initial residents of SABAH.city, contributing to a
vibrant community focused on innovation.

Phase 2: 2026-2029 - Scaling up and expanding capabilities
In the second phase, the developed area will expand to roughly 15 hectares, enabling the
construction of additional infrastructure and enhanced facilities. This phase will introduce
cutting-edge research centers, state-of-the-art data centers, and advanced co-working spaces
designed to foster collaboration and drive innovation. By this stage, SABAH.city will further
integrate leading educational institutions and innovative companies as foundational residents,
cementing its role as a dynamic environment for research and technological advancement.
Phase 3: 2029 and Beyond - further expansion and internationalization
In the final phase, SABAH.city will extend across the full 20 hectares, maximizing resource
utilization and spatial efficiency. This expansion will include welcoming leading international
companies seeking to establish regional offices, thereby expanding the city’s global reach.
With this growth, SABAH.city will solidify its position as a premier hub for research,
development, and innovation, attracting talent and investment from across the globe.
SABAH.city promises to be a landmark destination for innovation, bringing together
academia, industry, and investors in a collaborative environment that supports sustainable
growth and technological breakthroughs.

community for partnerships and growth opportunities.
• Startup and tech academies: Educational programs to develop essential skills for the
startup and tech industries.
• Technology transfer programs: Facilitating the transfer of cutting-edge technology from
research to market.

 Project Framework

The 2024 project is focused on a developing start-up and incubation programmes for the
High Technologies Park (HTP) and improving the technology commercialization system in
Azerbaijan.
This project began with a kick-off seminar and an extensive current status analysis. During
this phase, we formed a dedicated research team and outlined the scope of our research.
This part of the project consisted in investigating the local landscape by conducting a
thorough assessment of the existing start-up and incubation regulations in Azerbaijan, while
simultaneously examining the start-up and incubation services offered at HTP, pinpointing
areas that required further attention.
In the pursuit of enhancing capacity, a collabo rative effort with diverse experts and
organisations in South Korea was initiated. This collaboration included inviting Azerbaijan
experts for training and organi sing specialized workshops on technology transfer and
commercialization. These initiatives aimed to foster exchanges of knowledge and share
Korea’s experience and methodologies.
An integral aspect of the 2024 project involved the development of an online platform for
technology transfer and commercialization. This platform was designed to enhance the
ecosystem of technology transfer and commercialization in Azerbaijan by offering
information, incorporating enhancements in content and technological functionality, and
ensuring that it would serve as a valuable resource for stakeholders.
Finally, a forum has been conducted to disseminate the outcomes of the 2024 project. This
forum played a pivotal role in providing policy recommendations concerning technology
commercialization governance in Azerbaijan and boosting the roles of the HTP. The aim was
to ensure that the insights gained from capacity-building activities were not only shared but
also effectively disseminated to the relevant stakeholders, thereby contributing to the
broader ecosystem of technology transfer and commercialization in Azerbaijan.

 (Capacity-Building) Open Innovation Workshop
In light of the increasing demand for innovative policy-making capabilities, a broad spectrum
of diverse learning methodologies has emerged as a viable option in contemporary contexts.
One such methodology that warrants consideration is the workshop approach.
The Open Innovation Workshop was conceived and executed with the overarching objective
of aligning with the objectives of the 2024 project. This project's primary mission was to
fortify the operational prowess and competitive standing of the HTP (High Technologies
Park) through Implementation of the Application Process with the Implementation of
Capacity Building of the TC Start-up and Incubation System in Azerbaijan. Additionally, it
sought to facilitate the exchange and dissemination of knowledge among local stakeholders.
The Open Innovation Pla tform Workshop for the year 2024 represents an integral
component of the capacity -building initiative within the purview of the K -Innovation
Partnership Program. The workshop's ultimate aim was to furnish the participants with TC
start-up basic principles and illuminate the commercial dimensions of technology, to be
especially based on Korean I-Corps TTT(Train The Trainer) Program. The diverse participant
base included individuals from various professional backgrounds, including current and
prospective inventors, entrepreneurs, experts in technology commercialization and transfer,
as well as professionals affiliated with Technology Transfer Organizations (TTOs), among
others.
Through the strategic deployment of this workshop, it is anticipated that a multifaceted
approach encompassing learning, practical application, innovative discovery, and the
exchange of ideas will contribute significantly to the capacity-building efforts of ecosystem
stakeholders. As a result, this initiative is poised to catalyse improvements and
advancements within Azerbaijan's Technology Transfer (TT) and Technology
Commercialization (TC) landscape through training the instructors.

 Research Framework

A systematic review of existing academic literature on technology commercialization can
demonstrate the ambiguous role of the capabilities of technology transfer offices (TTO) in
transferring technologies from a university/research institute to industry. For example, Lee
and Jung (2021) identified three distinctive capabilities of technology commercialization (TC)
while analysing longitudinal data from South Korea for the period 2009-2015. These
capabilities include academic research capability, applied res earch capability, and TTO
operation capability. Their analysis of the effects of these capabilities on TC performance
reveals that, unlike the other two capabilities, the TTO operation capability has no significant
effect on TC performance. Therefore, establishing an effective operational capability alone
for a high-technology park cannot solve the challenges associated with TC activities. Min,
Kim and Vonortas (2020) also analysed the conditions for TC and commercial exploitation
of public technologies in Korea. Their analysis of 514 cases of TC by major public research
institutes and universities underline that market competition is a key determinant of
successful TC, and that it also affects business growth in general.
Moreover, regardless of the market situation, effective partnership is critical for successful
TC. Recently, Belitzki, Aginskaya and Marozau (2019) also identified the role of TTOs and
direct industrial funding in TC in the transition economies of Azerbaijan, Belarus and
Kazakhstan during 2015-2017. Interestingly, their research reveals that successful research
commercialization is neither associated with the existence and awareness of TTOs, nor with
their contracts with partners. However, direct industrial funding of research is a significant
factor.

◼ Policy-making issues
A successful innovation policy for a developing country requires careful consideration of a
wide range of activities. To achieve better TC results, policy makers should develop a
comprehensive innovation policy and then consider certain key issues when implementing
that policy. Such issues may include, but are not limited to, the fol lowing: the state of
innovation; strategic priorities; access to finance; intellectual property protection; capacity
building; and collaboration and cooperation.

◼ Institutions
TC is a process by which necessary measures are undertaken in order to pass a given
research output through the relevant procedures and make it beneficial for
commercialization in various applications. For TC to act effectively, several types of
institutions play important roles: government agencies; research centres and universities;
business incubators and accelerators; venture capital firms; and associations and consortia.

◼ Funding mechanisms
TC requires several funding mechanisms to be available. The range of such funding schemes
may vary depending on the stage of technology development, or on the goals of the
stakeholders wh o seek to commercialize such technologies. Various major funding
mechanisms also play an important role, including government grants, equity financing,
debt financing, corporate partnerships, and crowdfunding.

◼ Regulatory frameworks
Complying with legislation and regulations is a must-do requirement for TC. Therefore, it is
critical to assess regulatory frameworks that may impact it. There are several regulatory
frameworks that are particularly important for TC, and such frameworks - encompassing

across three strategic phases:
• Phase 1: 2024-2026 - Laying the foundations and building initial traction
During this foundational phase, SABAH.city will begin establishing initial facilities across
approximately 4 hectares, setting the stage for future growth. Key components, including
startup incubators, accelerators, venture studios, venture capital funds, and technology
transfer offices, will be operational, providing essential support for emerging businesses.
In addition, leading public and private educational institutions and pioneering companies
will relocate and establish themselves as the initial residents of SABAH.city, contributing
to a vibrant community focused on innovation.

construction of additional infrastructure and enhanced facilities. This phase will introduce
cutting-edge research centers, state-of-the-art data centers, and advanced co-working
spaces designed to foster collaboration and drive innovation. By this stage, SABAH.city
will further integrate leading educational institutions and innovative companies as
foundational residents, cementing its role as a dynamic environment for research and
technological advancement.

 Conclusion

This Report on the Status of the K-Innovation Partnership Program 2022-2024 between
STEPI and the HTP can be considered a follow-up to the previous status report submitted in
2022. This report mainly concentrates on the updates that have occurred since July 2022,
while considerable institutional reforms have been implemented within ANAS. Numerous
institutions, mostly in the field of natural and technical sciences, have been transferred
within the structure of the Ministry of Science and Education. As for ANAS, it mostly
manages institutes in humanitarian and social sciences. Most importantly for this project,
the ANAS HTP is no longer a unit of ANAS, although the interagency transferring process is
still ongoing and needs to be finalized.
Another significant event entails strengthening the authority of the Ministry of Transport
and Digital Development as a central power to govern the innovation ecosystem, digital
transformation, and e-governance. All these responsibilities are currently assigned to the
Innovation and Digital Development Agency. The HTP would benefit from closer
collaboration with the IDDA and active participation in the innovation ecosystem.
So far, Azerbaijan has not introduced any legal acts on competition, venture capital, etc.
Moreover, a draft of the national innovation strategy needs to be approved in order not to
miss the right timing or lose out on the market potential. Equity and debt financing, and
crowdfunding are underperforming forms of funding mechanisms in the country.
Furthermore, the unavailability of venture capital firms in the domestic market makes the
creation of more dynamic markets and financing an even more challenging task.
We believe that the process of re-formation of HTP will take place after it becomes part of
the Ministry of Science and Education. The visits of the minister and other management
staff to the HTP, and their respective ideas, indicate that the HTP will once again be formed
into a modern technology park.

NSF to develop various technologies that benefit society.

solve industrial problems.

products and services with social and economic impact. It includes programs like NSF I-Corps
(Innovation Corps), Partnerships for Innovation (PFI), and the America Seed Fund.

Ecosystems (POSE) program, which accelerates the economic and social potential of
projects born from basic science and engineering, creating benefits across the national
innovation ecosystem.
TIP focuses on bringing together researchers, practitioners, and users to determine research
directions, promote iterative co-design and co-creation, develop innovative technologies
and solutions for national social and economic challenges, and foster future talent.

instructors, ③ professionals such as patent attorneys, lawyers, and accountants, and ④
highly experienced individuals with business or industry experience.

the selection target for instructors has been shifted from university professors to industry-
specific mentors since 2023.

Instructor, Instructor, and Pre-Instructor. This system operates similarly to a promotion
system, where industry-specific mentors are granted Pre-Instructor qualifications and can

1.2.1.2 Training Program Culture
Instructors push, challenge, and question teams to learn quickly due to time constraints.
Instructors are direct, open, and rigorous in conducting the program, similar to the real
world, but the main purpose of the training is to help understand the Lean Startup process.
Therefore, comments from instructors and mentors should not be taken as criticism of
individuals or teams but as part of the process of learning about the process, market, and
oneself.
Instructors also expect teams to ask questions, challenge their perspectives if they disagree,
and engage in real conversations with the education team. This approach may sometimes
feel harsh or abrupt, but it is intended to help teams learn to challenge themselves quickly
and objectively and understand that they need to learn and evolve incredibly fast as
entrepreneurs.
The education team strives to start and end on time, so punctuality is strictly enforced.
Therefore, all participating teams should carefully check the time allocated for
presentations and may face disadvantages for being late or unprepared.
Participants must enter interview notes, revise the Business Model Canvas, and
communicate with instructors by the scheduled time using online task support tools and
templates such as Box.
Attendance at all sessions is mandatory, and the only exception for not attending a session
is a critical customer discovery interview that cannot be scheduled at another time, except
for serious illness. Absences for other reasons must be discussed with the instructors and
mentors and approved in advance by the program director or instructors.

Chapter 5. Conclusion
115

The implementation of the application process, along with capacity building within the TT
and TC systems in Azerbaijan, has been a significant milestone in fostering entrepreneurship
and innovation in the country. This project has not only addressed the critical gaps in the
ecosystem but has also paved the way for a sustainable and robust infrastructure to support
emerging businesses. By integrating Korean best practices with local insights, the initiative
has successfully created a foundation for fostering innovation, increasing economic
diversification, and promoting the development of a knowledge-based economy.
One of the project's key achievements has been the establishment of a streamlined,
transparent, and accessible application process. By implementing digital tools and platforms,
the application process was made more efficient, user-friendly, and inclusive. This digital
transformation has enabled aspiring entrepreneurs to submit their proposals and access
incubation services regardless of their geographical location. Additionally, the standardized
evaluation criteria and merit-based selection processes ensured that the most promising
ideas and ventures were given the opportunity to flourish.
The application process's refinement has also contributed to reducing barriers to entry for
underrepresented groups, including women, youth, and individuals from rural areas. This
inclusivity has been instrumental in fostering a diverse pool of innovators, enriching the
ecosystem with varied perspectives and ideas. Furthermore, the integration of feedback
mechanisms allowed the system to evolve continuously, addressing user concerns and
adapting to emerging trends.
The project’s emphasis on capacity building has been pivotal in enhancing the skills and
competencies of stakeholders within the start-up and TC ecosystem. Training programs,
workshops, and mentorship initiatives were organized to equip entrepreneurs, incubator
managers, and policymakers with the knowledge and tools required to succeed in their respective
