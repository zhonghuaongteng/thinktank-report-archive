gas. Azerbaijan is hugely reliant on the oil and gas industry, which accounts for more than
90% of its total exports, and has an economic structure trade that is contingent upon three
neighbouring countries, namely, Russia, Iran, and Turkey. In other words, it is a country that
is economically and socially affected by unstable fluctuations in international oil prices and
by changes in the circumstances of neighbouring countries.
Recently, Azerbaijan’s government resolved to move away from excessive dependence on
the oil industry and make the transition to a knowledge-based economy, in a bid to avoid a
financial crisis caused by unstable oil prices and to create a democratic political environment.
It is in this context that the government established 'Azerbaijan 2020: Look into the Future’
and 'Azerbaijan 2030: National Priorities for Socio-Economic Development' with the aim of
achieving sustainable economic development based on innovation in the field of science
and technology. The creation of the Azerbaijan National Cooperation Strategy (2016) shows
that the government is making efforts to strengthen the country’s science and technology
innovation capacity under the national development plan and to implement industrial
diversification policies aimed at building national competitiveness. In addition, it
emphasizes the need to increase investment in science and technology innovation and to
improve the operation of its system by increasing the efficiency of knowledge creation,
technology transfer, and the application and commercialization of technology through the
'Azerbaijan 2030 Strategic Roadmap'.
