Summary
 — Digital services are critical to the management of government budgets, 
the modernization of societies and economic growth. This transformation has 
the potential to boost efficiency, reduce spending, and improve accessibility 
and inclusion for citizens. However, to date, efforts in this direction have been 
characterized by high costs, long-term dependence on foreign service providers 
and siloed projects that result in duplication and waste. Creating a digital 
society that can unlock new public goods requires an urgent and comprehensive 
reset of strategic thinking and approach.
 — Digital public infrastructure (DPI) is a way of building society-scale digital 
systems that can overcome some of the past issues encountered in the 
digitalization process by adhering to a few key principles. These include only 
initiating open, public-interest digital solutions that are interoperable with 
other systems, that can be built upon and that enable the secure sharing of data. 
Common examples of DPI include digital identity systems, digital payment 
systems and data exchange systems. The term DPI refers both to the approach 
and principles behind open, interoperable digitalization, and to the resulting 
digital systems and platforms that add infrastructural capability. 
 — The principles of DPI offer five key benefits for governments looking to build 
robust digital foundations:
 — Sovereignty: DPI approaches aim to counter foreign dependencies and 
vendor lock-in, while allowing more scope for national principles and values 
to shape and inform society-scale technology.
 — Economic opportunity: By replacing expensive, proprietary and siloed 
digital services with open, interoperable components that can share data, 
DPI can increase bureaucratic efficiency, boost access to credit, improve 
tax collection, lower costs for businesses, and provide a foundation for 
local innovation.
 — Resilience and security: DPI can safeguard national security by reducing 
reliance on foreign-controlled proprietary systems, as well as by ensuring 
that critical digital infrastructure remains sovereign, auditable and resilient 
against geopolitical coercion or cyberattacks.
 — Public sector modernization: Digital public service delivery is proven 
to boost efficiency and user satisfaction. DPI can facilitate a ‘whole-of-
government’ approach that securely enables government departments 
to share data, and citizens to access a wide range of services through 
a single interface.
