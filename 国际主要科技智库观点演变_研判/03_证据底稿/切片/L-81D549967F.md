Role and Challenges of Small Businesses from the Perspective of
Transformative Innovation Policy
Jieun Seong· Hyejin Jo· Wichin Song
Small businesses are being reexamined as key agents of regional innovation and
national growth amid complex crises including low birthrates, aging populations,
regional decline, and structural low growth. As small businesses are increasingly
included as major beneficiaries of AI and digital transformation policies, a paradigm
shift toward strengthening innovation capabilities has begun in earnest. This report
presents policy challenges for strengthening the role of small businesses and their
innovation capabilities from the perspective of transformative innovation policy.
Recently administration has redefined small businesses as economic, cultural,
and social innovation a gents and established related policy foundations. Under the
‘People’s Sovereignty Government’ initiative, small businesses are positioned as ‘core
agents of economic sovereignty’ and ‘public and social agents maintaining the
community safety net’, expanding their role beyond simple protection and support
targets. Institutional foundations have also been established, including the designation
of small businesses as AI support targets in Article 16 of the AI Basic Act, AI and digital
support system construction under National Agenda No. 61, and the establishment of a
dedicated vice-ministerial office for small business policy within the Ministry of SMEs
and Startups. While the administration’s small business policies show positive
direction from a transformati ve innovation policy perspective - particularly in
redefining small businesses as innovation agents and pursuing differentiated policies
reflecting regional characteristics - fragmented support systems across ministries,
limitations of individual firm -centered support, and the need to transition policy
outcomes toward tangible social and economic impact remain 30 Summary as key
challenges.
