Summary
China’s 2025 retirement reform creates a temporary statutory working-age window rather
than a solution to population aging. Using a monthly birth-cohort simulation, this report
estimates the reform’s effect through 2050 and examines what would be required for the
additional statutory headcount to become productive economic capacity. In the preferred
scenario, the reform keeps the statutory working-age population broadly stable through the mid-
2030s and leaves about 48 million more people below statutory retirement thresholds in 2035
than under the old rules. Its direct contribution to growth is limited, however, and its broader
value depends on older-worker participation, employer demand, productivity, service-sector
absorption, and how artificial intelligence affects the occupations represented among the retained
cohorts.
• The reform creates a temporary statutory working-age window, not a solution to aging. In
the preferred central scenario, it holds China’s statutory working-age population roughly
flat through the mid-2030s—a rise of about 5.5 million between 2024 and 2035, against a
decline of roughly 43 million under the old rules. Relative to the old rules, it keeps about
28 million additional people below the statutory retirement threshold by 2030 and about
48 million by 2035.
• The period of broad stability ends after the mid-2030s; the reform effect itself remains
large into the 2040s. The reform delays the decline in the working-age population but
does not reverse it. The magnitude of the buffering effect peaks in the early-to-mid 2040s
at roughly 66 to 79 million additional people depending on specification, and then it
erodes as the statutory population resumes a sustained decline. This quantity refers to
statutory headcount, not realized employment.
• The cushion depends disproportionately on extending the working lives of blue-collar
women. Women account for roughly 54 percent of the 2035 effect. Blue-collar women on
the lower-wage “old-50” track, who receive the reform’s largest increase of five years,
supply about 38 percent of the total.
• The reform’s direct contribution to growth is small because labor had already stopped
driving Chinese economic growth. Over 2016–2023, labor contributed essentially nothing
to GDP growth while capital and productivity carried almost all of it. Even on the
generous assumption that every retained person works, the reform would leave annual
growth through 2035 only about 0.3 percentage points higher than it would be under the
old rules. This is a minuscule number relative to the roughly 4.17 percent average annual
GDP growth target that Beijing has identified as necessary to achieve its 2035 income
target.
• Whether the window becomes true economic capacity depends on productivity, demand,
and AI’s effect on the retained workers. Meeting the 2035 target increasingly falls on
productivity as capital’s contribution fades. Many women assigned to the old-50 proxy
