Determinants of Earnings among Recent STEM PhD Graduates in
Korea and Policy Implications
Seungyoon Lee, Mi-Jung Um

 Recent science and engineering PhD graduates are core resources for
national R&D competitiveness; understanding their compensation structure
and early-career earnings is essential
 Science and engineering PhD holders play central roles in innovation by conducting
R&D and training the nex t generation of researchers; their effective training and
utilization underpin national R&D performance
 The doctoral program is a high -cost human capital investment requiring long -term
intensive investment from both individuals and the nation, necessitat ing the
establishment of efficient investment directions to ensure appropriate compensation
in the labor market

 This study provides empirical evidence relevant for doctoral training and
STEM workforce policy by analyzing the correlates of earnings among recent
science and engineering PhD graduates
 We examine associations between earnings and field of study, doctoral program
characteristics, research outputs, and research experiences, and document the
magnitude of earnings differentials across groups
