 Global South: A Strategic Partner for Korea in Responding to Shifts in the
Global Economic and Technological Landscape
 Background and Rationale
- The international environment is undergoing a profound transformation, driven in
part by intensifying competition for technological leadership between the United
States and China. In this context, Korea faces an urgent need to redefine its role in the
global community and to strengthen its capacity for strategic risk management.
- While Korea’s long -standing framework of security cooperation with the United
States and economic interdependence with China is unlikely to change rapidly, Korea
requires multidimensional mid- to long-term strategies to secure new growth engines,
enhance national innovation capabilities, and contribute effectively to sustainable
development.
- Korea is particularly well positioned to assume the role of a middle-power leader for
inclusive global innovation, given its dual experience in: science - and technology-
driven economic development, and development cooperation and assistance.
 Why the Global South Matters
- The Global South has emerged as an increasingly critical strategic partner for Korea,
particularly in the context of science, technology, and innovation (STI) cooperation.
This is underpinned by several structural factors:
- Demographics: A young population base and an expanding working-age population
are expected to strengthen long-term growth potential.

 Analytical Approach
- The report identifies countries with high potential for strategic STI cooperation by
applying comparative analysis across indicators related to economic and
political/social foundations, STI capabilities, and domain - specific capacities for
responding to global challenges (climate, digital, health).
- From the 133 G77 countries, 13 countries across regions (Latin America, Africa, Asia,
Middle East/Europe) were selected based on existing literature and assessed using
international indicators. Cou ntries were comparatively analyzed along two
dimensions: Economic growth and cooperation capacity, and Science, technology, and
innovation (STI) capacity
- Based on this framework, four country groups were identified, each requiring
differentiated cooperation strategies.

and joint leadership.
